Defined terms Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations Overview This chapter deals with the requirements of IFRS 5 Non-current Assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations. As its title suggests, the standard deals with two distinct but overlapping broad areas. The first relates to the measurement and presentation of non-current assets when these will be principally recovered by disposal rather than by continuing use. The requirements deal with, inter alia: • Classification as held for sale (and changes to a plan to sell) • The ceasing of depreciation and amortisation • The writing down of an asset and group of assets and liabilities to be disposed of to the lower of their carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell And • The separate presentation of such assets and liabilities in the statement of financial position The second matter relates to the concept of a discontinued operation. Requirements in this regard cover the identification of and presentation in profit and loss of the results of discontinued operations, the representation of comparative information to reflect the status of the operation at the end of the current period and cash flow information relating to the operation. The standard sets out disclosure requirements in relation to the above. What you need to know • IFRS 5 sets out the criteria for classification of an asset and group of assets and liabilities as held for sale and the definition of discontinued operations, as well as corresponding measurement, presentation and disclosure requirements. • The standard by design requires often significant judgements, based on the specific facts and circumstances applicable to an entity. • Various matters relating to the standard have been raised over the years and discussed by the IFRS IC which deferred them to the IASB, such as the allocation of impairments in a disposal group or the treatment of intragroup transactions between continuing and discontinued operations. To date the IASB has not performed a post-implementation review of IFRS 5 nor otherwise addressed these matters. • Entities need to carefully consider disclosing information needed to evaluate the financial effects of discontinued operations and disposals of non-current assets (or disposal groups), including the basis for and the effects of significant judgements and accounting policy choices made based on the entity’s specific facts and circumstances. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations Objective and scope of IFRS 5 The objective of IFRS 5 is to specify the accounting for assets held for sale, and the presentation and disclosure of discontinued operations. In particular, the standard requires that non-current assets (and, in a ‘disposal group’, related liabilities and current assets, discussed in section 2.1.1 below) meeting its criteria to be classified as held for sale be: (a) Measured at the lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell, with depreciation (or amortisation) on them ceasing And (b) Presented separately on the face of the statement of financial position with the results of discontinued operations presented separately in the statement of comprehensive income [IFRS 5.1] The classification and presentation requirements apply to all recognised non-current assets and disposal groups, while there are certain exceptions to the measurement provisions of the standard. [IFRS 5.2, IFRS 5.5]. These issues are discussed further in section 2.2 below. The classification, presentation and measurement requirements of IFRS 5 applicable to assets (or disposal groups) classified as held for sale also apply to those classified as held for distribution to owners acting in their capacity as owners. [IFRS 5.5A]. This is discussed in section 2.1.2 below. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations Non-current assets (and disposal groups) held for sale or held for distribution to owners 2.1 Classification of non-current assets (and disposal groups) held for sale or held for distribution to owners IFRS 5 frequently refers to current assets and non-current assets. It provides a definition of each term as follows: [IFRS 5 Appendix A] Extract from IFRS 5 Appendix A Defined terms current asset An entity shall classify an asset as current when: (a) it expects to realise the asset, or intends to sell or consume it, in its normal operating cycle; (b) it holds the asset primarily for the purpose of trading; (c) it expects to realise the asset within twelve months after the reporting period; or (d) the asset is cash or a cash equivalent (as defined in IAS 7) unless the asset is restricted from being exchanged or used to settle a liability for at least twelve months after the reporting period. non-current asset An asset that does not meet the definition of a current asset. disposal group A group of assets to be disposed of, by sale or otherwise, together as a group in a single transaction, and liabilities directly associated with those assets that will be transferred in the transaction. The group includes goodwill acquired in a business combination if the group is a cash-generating unit to which goodwill has been allocated in accordance with the requirements of paragraphs 80-87 of IAS 36 Impairment of Assets (as revised in 2004) or if it is an operation within such a cash-generating unit. The first two definitions are the same as those in IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements (discussed in # Chapter 3 at section 3.1.1). 2.1.1 The concept of a disposal group As its title suggests, IFRS 5 addresses the accounting treatment of non-current assets held for sale, that is assets whose carrying amount will be recovered principally through sale rather than continuing use in the business. [IFRS 5.6]. However, the standard also applies to certain liabilities and current assets where they form part of a ‘disposal group’. The standard observes that sometimes an entity will dispose of a group of assets, possibly with some directly associated liabilities, together in a single transaction. [IFRS 5.4]. A common example would be the disposal of a subsidiary. For these circumstances, IFRS 5 introduces the concept of a disposal group, which it defines as a group of assets to be disposed of, by sale or otherwise, together as a group in a single transaction, and liabilities directly associated with those assets that will be transferred in the transaction. The group includes goodwill acquired in a business combination if the group is a cash-generating unit to which goodwill has been allocated in accordance with the requirements of IAS 36 Impairment of Assets (discussed in Chapter 21) or if it is an operation within such a cash-generating unit. [IFRS 5 Appendix A]. The use of the phrase ‘together in a single transaction’ indicates that the only liabilities that can be included in the group are those assumed by the purchaser. Accordingly, any borrowings of the entity which are to be repaid out of the sales proceeds would be excluded from the disposal group. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations The standard goes on to explain that a disposal group: • May be a group of cash-generating units, a single cash-generating unit, or part of a cash-generating unit. Once the cash flows from an asset or group of assets are expected to arise principally from sale rather than continuing use, they become less dependent on cash flows arising from other assets, and a disposal group that was part of a cash-generating unit becomes a separate cash-generating unit. And • May include any assets and any liabilities of the entity, including current assets, current liabilities and assets outside the scope of the measurement requirements of IFRS 5 (see section 2.2 below). [IFRS 5.4]. Discontinued operations are discussed in section 3 below. As noted there, it seems highly unlikely that the definition of a discontinued operation would ever be met by a single non-current asset. Accordingly, a discontinued operation will normally include one or more disposal groups, while a disposal group oftentimes will not meet the definition of a discontinued operation. Also, while discontinued operations involve clearly distinguishable cash flows and a major line of business or major geographical area, none of those characteristics need to be present in disposal groups. 2.1.1.A Deferred tax assets and liabilities in a disposal group held for sale There may be various deferred tax assets and liabilities recorded in association with items included within a disposal group, such as deferred taxes on temporary differences between the tax base and the carrying amount of individual assets and liabilities included in a disposal group, or deferred tax assets on unused tax losses. It follows from the definition of a disposal group that an entity only classifies as held for sale those assets and liabilities that will be transferred to the buyer. [IFRS 5 Appendix A]. The standard does not include any further guidance on whether or when deferred tax assets and liabilities shall be included within a disposal group held for sale. How we see it In our view, it would be consistent with the definition of a disposal group to include deferred tax assets and liabilities within a disposal group if they will be assumed (taken over) by the buyer. However, where the disposal is expected to trigger a reversal of temporary differences related to assets and liabilities retained in the disposal group, any related deferred tax assets and liabilities would be retained (and ultimately settled) by the seller and must therefore be excluded from the disposal group. Whilst any assessment will have to reflect the specific facts and circumstances of the expected disposal transaction and the tax regulations in the specific jurisdictions involved, deferred taxes will often be assumed by the buyer if the disposal group is a separate legal entity and the disposal will be carried out by way of a ‘share deal’ (i.e., the sale of shares in the entity). In such a case, in many jurisdictions the tax bases of the individual assets and liabilities (sometimes referred to as the ‘tax history’) will be retained in the legal entity being sold and thereby will be transferred to the buyer. These deferred tax assets and liabilities shall therefore be included in the disposal group. [IFRS 5 Appendix A, IAS 12.51A]. Care must also be taken to ensure that any ‘outside’ temporary differences related to the entity being sold (i.e., deferred taxes related to a temporary difference between the carrying amount of the seller’s investment in the legal entity and the tax base of its investment) are not included in the disposal group, as this represents a liability or asset of the seller. ‘Outside’ temporary differences are discussed in Chapter 29 at section 7.6. If a group of individual assets and liabilities are being sold (i.e., the disposal will be carried out by way of an ‘asset deal’), the sale is likely to be treated by the tax authorities as a recovery of the related assets (or settlement of the related liabilities) and give rise to a reversal of previous temporary differences for the seller. A new tax base of those assets and liabilities for the buyer will usually be based on the purchase price. The seller will settle (realise) the deferred taxes on a disposal and will therefore not include these deferred tax balances in the disposal group. [IFRS 5 Appendix A, IAS 12.51A]. The form of the anticipated disposal (‘share deal’ or ‘asset deal’) will often be considered a significant element of the plan to sell. As discussed in section 2.1.2.B below, for the sale to be highly probable, significant changes to the plan shall be unlikely. [IFRS 5.8]. Therefore, if an entity finds it difficult to establish whether an anticipated sale will be carried out by way of a share deal or an asset deal, it may indicate that the held for sale criteria have not been met. Subject to specific facts and circumstances, the form of the disposal may be relevant for determining which assets and liabilities are to be classified as held for sale, as highlighted in the Illustration 2-1 below. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations Illustration 2-1: Deferred tax liability on remeasurement of an investment property held for sale Entity A, which operates in the real estate industry, is committed to a plan to sell a shopping mall that is held by its fully-owned subsidiary. The only assets of the subsidiary are the investment property itself, trade receivables and cash. The subsidiary has no liabilities, except for a deferred tax liability reflecting the difference between the carrying amount of the investment property and its tax base (which equals to the costs directly attributable to the construction of the shopping mall). Depending on whether the disposal is planned in the form of a share or asset deal, the scope of the disposal group will be impacted, as illustrated by Scenario 1 and Scenario 2 below. 1. Management of Entity A intends to dispose of the investment property by way of a share deal. The disposal group meets all the criteria to be classified as held for sale (see section 2.1.2 below). In the jurisdiction in which Entity A operates, no taxes are levied on sale of shares. The deferred tax liability will be transferred to the buyer, along with all the other assets of the subsidiary. Therefore, the entity includes the deferred tax liability of the subsidiary in the disposal group held for sale. 2. Management of Entity A intends to dispose of the investment property by way of an asset deal. On the disposal, the subsidiary will derecognise the investment property and realise the deferred tax liability associated with the investment property. The deferred tax liability is therefore excluded from the disposal group. The two scenarios above significantly impact the scope of the disposal group (assuming that the deferred tax liability of the subsidiary is material), and therefore an intention to realise the disposal through a share or an asset deal needs to be carefully considered before concluding whether held for sale classification is appropriate. A mere intention will often not be sufficient to conclude that a change in the form of the disposal is unlikely. Any income tax effect of the disposal is accounted for under IAS 12 Income Taxes and is not included in costs to sell when measuring the disposal group held for sale, as discussed in section 2.2.2.A below. An intention to dispose of a group of assets or a subsidiary undertaking can affect the measurement of deferred tax assets and liabilities before the proposed transaction gives rise to a reclassification of those related assets and liabilities into a disposal group under IFRS 5. For example, the measurement of deferred tax assets and liabilities may have to be reassessed because the manner of recovery is expected to be through sale rather than continuing use. [IAS 12.51]. This is discussed in Chapter 29 at section 7.5.8 (in the context of the recognition of deferred tax assets) and in section 8.4.11 (in the context of expected manner of recovery of an asset or settlement of liability). Any income tax gain or loss on disposal of a disposal group which represents a discontinued operation is presented within discontinuing operations (see section 3.2 below). 2.1.2 Classification as held for sale or as held for distribution to owners IFRS 5 requires a non-current asset (or disposal group) to be classified as held for sale if its carrying amount will be recovered principally through a sale transaction rather than through continuing use. [IFRS 5.6]. For these purposes, sale transactions include exchanges of non-current assets for other non- current assets when the exchange has commercial substance in accordance with IAS 16 Property, Plant and Equipment (discussed in Chapter 19 at section 4.4). [IFRS 5.10]. For assets classified according to a liquidity presentation (see Chapter 3 at section 3.1.1), non-current assets are taken to be assets that include amounts expected to be recovered more than twelve months after the reporting date. [IFRS 5.2]. Determining whether (and when) an asset stops being recovered principally through continuing use and becomes recoverable principally through sale is the critical distinction, and much of the standard is devoted to explaining how to make the determination. For an asset (or disposal group) to be classified as held for sale: (a) It must be available for immediate sale in its present condition, subject only to terms that are usual and customary for sales of such assets (or disposal groups) (b) Its sale must be highly probable [IFRS 5.7] And (c) the disposal must be expected to qualify for recognition as a completed sale within one year from the date of classification [IFRS 5.8]. These criteria are discussed further below. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations If an asset (or disposal group) has been classified as held for sale, but these criteria cease to be met, an entity must cease to classify the asset (or disposal group) as held for sale. [IFRS 5.26]. Changes in disposal plans are discussed in section 2.2.5 below. The reclassification from held for use to held for sale results from changes in relevant facts and circumstances. Often, a specific event will trigger held for sale classification, such as a formalised management decision or the receipt of a binding purchase offer, in which case the classification as held for sale must reflect the timing of the triggering event (assuming all of the criteria listed above are met, as also discussed further in sections 2.1.2.A, 2.1.2.B and 2.1.2.C below). It follows that the criteria for classification as held for sale are assessed throughout the reporting period. When these criteria are met at a specific point in time during the reporting period, a non-current asset (or disposal group) is reclassified from held for use and IFRS 5 measurement requirements (as discussed in section 2.2 below) apply from that date onwards, including that a non-current asset (or a disposal group) is remeasured to fair value less costs to sell and non-current assets cease to be depreciated or amortized. See also section 2.1.2.B for discussion on how external evidence (such as letters of intent, a binding offer or a firm purchase commitments) may impact the assessment of the highly probable criterion. Illustration 2-2: Timing of reclassification to held for sale The management of an international multi-industry group considers disposal of its German subsidiary. The subsidiary is available for immediate sale in its present condition. No regulatory approval is required. Financial year-end is 31 December and the Group issues half-year interim financial statements. The following summarises the relevant chain of events in 20X1: ## February 20X1 Management began to investigate whether disposal of the German subsidiary represented a realistic alternative, including feasibility studies and financial analyses. ## April 20X1 The disposal plan was formally approved by the shareholders and management began active marketing of the subsidiary. Given the scale of the German subsidiary, the complexity of the potential transaction and the economic outlook at the time, management assessed that the sale was unlikely (and therefore not highly probable) to be closed before the end of June 20X2. ## June 20X1 Management reviewed the above assumptions for the purposes of half-year reporting. No significant changes in facts and circumstances were identified. July - September 20X1 A few non-binding offers were received and discussions were held with potential buyers. None of these events led management to assess that the disposal was highly probable within the next 12 months. ## October 20X1 A binding purchase offer was received. The management considered all the facts and circumstances and assessed that completion of the sale within the next 12 months became highly probable. In this fact pattern, the German subsidiary was classified as held for sale as at 1 October 20X1 being the first date at which all criteria were met, including the one year criterion. Slightly different criteria apply when an entity acquires a non-current asset (or disposal group) exclusively with a view to its subsequent disposal. In that case it must only classify the non-current asset (or disposal group) as held for sale at the acquisition date if: • The ‘one-year rule’ is met subject to its one exception (this is part of being ‘highly probable’, discussed in section 2.1.2.B below) And • It is highly probable that any other criteria in (a) and (b) above that are not met at that date will be met within a short period following the acquisition (usually within three months) [IFRS 5.11] The standard also makes it clear that the criteria above must be met at the reporting date for a non-current asset (or disposal group) to be classified as held for sale in those financial statements. However, if those criteria are met after the reporting date but before the authorisation of the financial statements for issue, the standard requires certain additional disclosures (discussed in section 5 below). [IFRS 5.12]. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations The classification, presentation and measurement requirements of IFRS 5 applicable to non-current assets (or disposal groups) classified as held for sale also apply to those classified as held for distribution to owners acting in their capacity as owners. [IFRS 5.5A]. This applies when an entity is committed to distribute the non-current asset (or disposal group) to its owners. For this to be the case, the assets must be available for immediate distribution in their present condition and the distribution must be highly probable. 2.1.2.A Meaning of available for immediate sale To qualify for classification as held for sale, a non-current asset (or disposal group) must be available for immediate sale in its present condition subject only to terms that are usual and customary for sales of such assets (or disposal groups). This is taken to mean that an entity currently has the intention and ability to transfer the asset (or disposal group) to a buyer in its present condition. The standard illustrates this concept with the following examples. [IFRS 5.IG Example 1, IFRS 5.IG Example 2, IFRS 5.IG Example 3]. Illustration 2-3: Meaning of ‘available for immediate sale’ Disposal of a headquarters building An entity is committed to a plan to sell its headquarters building and has initiated actions to locate a buyer. (a) The entity intends to transfer the building to a buyer after it vacates the building. The time necessary to vacate the building is usual and customary for sales of such assets. The criterion of being available for immediate sale would therefore be met at the plan commitment date. (b) The entity will continue to use the building until construction of a new headquarters building is completed. The entity does not intend to transfer the existing building to a buyer until after construction of the new building is completed (and it vacates the existing building). The delay in the timing of the transfer of the existing building imposed by the entity (seller) demonstrates that the building is not available for immediate sale. The criterion would not be met until construction of the new building is completed, even if a firm purchase commitment for the future transfer of the existing building is obtained earlier. Sale of a manufacturing facility An entity is committed to a plan to sell a manufacturing facility and has initiated actions to locate a buyer. At the plan commitment date, there is a backlog of uncompleted customer orders. (a) The entity intends to sell the manufacturing facility with its operations. Any uncompleted customer orders at the sale date will be transferred to the buyer. The transfer of uncompleted customer orders at the sale date will not affect the timing of the transfer of the facility. The criterion of being available for immediate sale would therefore be met at the plan commitment date. (b) The entity intends to sell the manufacturing facility, but without its operations. The entity does not intend to transfer the facility to a buyer until after it ceases all operations of the facility and eliminates the backlog of uncompleted customer orders. The delay in the timing of the transfer of the facility imposed by the entity (seller) demonstrates that the facility is not available for immediate sale. The criterion would not be met until the operations of the facility cease, even if a firm purchase commitment for the future transfer of the facility were obtained earlier. Land and buildings acquired through foreclosure An entity acquires through foreclosure a property comprising land and buildings that it intends to sell. (a) The entity does not intend to transfer the property to a buyer until after it completes renovations to increase the property’s sales value. The delay in the timing of the transfer of the property imposed by the entity (seller) demonstrates that the property is not available for immediate sale. The criterion of being available for immediate sale would therefore not be met until the renovations are completed. (b) After the renovations are completed and the property is classified as held for sale but before a firm purchase commitment is obtained, the entity becomes aware of environmental damage requiring remediation. The entity still intends to sell the property. However, the entity does not have the ability to transfer the property to a buyer until after the remediation is completed. The delay in the timing of the transfer of the property imposed by others before a firm purchase commitment is obtained demonstrates that the property is not available for immediate sale (different requirements could apply if this happened after a firm commitment is obtained, as illustrated in scenario (b) of Illustration 2-5 in section 2.1.2.B below). The criterion that the asset be available for immediate sale would not continue to be met. The property would be reclassified as held and used in accordance with the requirements discussed in section 2.2.5 below. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations There may be circumstances other than those illustrated above where a non-current asset (or disposal group) is not available for immediate sale. A common example is when the expected transaction requires legal reorganizations before it can be executed (e.g., a carve out of activities in the disposal plan from continuing activities). Whether a non-current asset (or disposal group) is available for immediate sale will generally be obvious. However, this assessment will sometimes require a careful consideration of all facts and circumstances and involve some degree of judgement. When significant judgement is involved and this has a material impact on the financial statements, appropriate disclosures should be provided in the notes. [IAS 1.122]. 2.1.2.B Meaning of highly probable Many observers may consider the meaning of ‘highly probable’ to be reasonably self-evident, albeit highly judgemental. However, IFRS 5 provides extensive discussion of the topic. As a first step, the term is defined by the standard as meaning ”significantly more likely than probable”. This is supplemented by a second definition - ‘probable’ is defined as ”more likely than not”. [IFRS 5 Appendix A]. Substituting the latter into the former leads to a definition of highly probable as meaning ‘significantly more likely than more likely than not’. The standard goes on to elaborate as follows: For the sale to be highly probable: • The appropriate level of management must be committed to a plan to sell the asset (or disposal group). • An active programme to locate a buyer and complete the plan must have been initiated. • The asset (or disposal group) must be actively marketed for sale at a price that is reasonable in relation to its current fair value. • The sale must be expected to qualify for recognition as a completed sale within one year from the date of classification (although in certain circumstances this period may be extended as discussed below). And • Actions required to complete the plan must indicate that it is unlikely that significant changes to the plan will be made or that the plan will be withdrawn. [IFRS 5.8]. In September 2013, the IFRS IC published an agenda decision in which it observed that the first three items above must have occurred to allow classification as held for sale. ## The other criteria would be assessed based on expectations of the future with their probability of occurrence included in the assessment of whether a sale is highly probable. The assessment can be highly judgemental and requires analysis of all relevant facts and circumstances, including the assessment of probability of obtaining shareholders’ approval, if relevant. [IFRS 5.8] Illustration 2-4: Shareholders’ approval The board has approved a plan to sell a manufacturing facility and has initiated actions to identify a buyer before the end of the reporting period. In accordance with the articles of association, formal approval by a majority of shareholders is required to finalise the sale. The next shareholders meeting is scheduled after the end of the reporting period. The following two scenarios illustrate how shareholder approvals may impact the assessment of whether the highly probable criterion is met under different circumstances: (a) The shareholding is widely dispersed and the rationale for the sale is assumed to potentially be controversial. In this scenario, the board would generally not have sufficient evidence to conclude that it is reasonable to expect that a majority of shareholders will vote for the sale. Therefore, the entity concludes that the highly probable criterion is not met. (b) Before the end of the reporting period, the management had discussions with the majority shareholder who clearly suggested that it would vote in favour of the disposal. In such a scenario, there may be a basis to conclude that the highly probable criterion is met, albeit that depends on facts and circumstances. In particular, if the management is provided with a written consent from the majority shareholder before the end of the reporting period, that would normally be considered sufficient to conclude that the highly probable criterion is met (absent any other circumstances which would indicate otherwise). IFRIC update, September 2013, available on the IFRS Foundation’s website. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations An entity needs to consider all relevant facts and circumstances (including for example communication by the entity outside the financial statements) and apply judgement when assessing whether and when a sale becomes highly probable. The standard does not require specific evidence for the highly probable criterion to be met but the hurdle is generally high and a mere intention to sell an asset would not trigger a classification as held for sale. Therefore, the lack of external evidence such as letters of intent, a binding offer or a firm purchase commitment often indicates that the criterion is not met. Conversely, even if a firm purchase commitment is obtained before the end of the reporting period, the entity can still conclude in some cases that the criterion is not met if other facts and circumstances cast doubt on whether the sale will be finalised - for instance, if a required regulatory approval is highly uncertain. As noted above, the classification, presentation and measurement requirements of IFRS 5 applicable to non-current assets (or disposal groups) classified as held for sale also apply to those classified as held for distribution to owners acting in their capacity as owners (see section 2.1.2 above). [IFRS 5.5A]. For the distribution to be highly probable, actions to complete the distribution must have been initiated and must be expected to be completed within one year from the date of classification. Actions required to complete the distribution must indicate that it is unlikely that significant changes to the distribution will be made or that the distribution will not be completed. Whilst judgement will be needed in individual circumstances, relevant actions to consider could include: the steps taken by management to prepare for the distribution, board decisions illustrating the commitment to the planned distribution, and steps taken to organise the meeting of shareholders, if their approval is required. The probability of shareholders’ approval, if this is required, must be considered as part of the assessment of whether the distribution is highly probable. [IFRS 5.12A]. The basic rule above that for qualification as held for sale the sale must be expected to qualify for recognition as a completed sale within one year from the date of classification (the ‘one year rule’) is applied quite strictly by the standard. [IFRS 5.IG Example 4]. Extract from IFRS 5 - Guidance on implementing Completion of sale expected within one year (paragraph 8) Example 4 To qualify for classification as held for sale, the sale of a non-current asset (or disposal group) must be highly probable (paragraph 7), and transfer of the asset (or disposal group) must be expected to qualify for recognition as a completed sale within one year (paragraph 8). That criterion would not be met if, for example: (a) an entity that is a commercial leasing and finance company is holding for sale or lease equipment that has recently ceased to be leased and the ultimate form of a future transaction (sale or lease) has not yet been determined. (b) an entity is committed to a plan to ‘sell’ a property that is in use as part of a sale and leaseback transaction, but the transfer does not qualify to be accounted for as a sale in accordance with paragraph 99 of IFRS 16 Leases and, instead, will be accounted for in accordance with paragraph 103 of IFRS 16. In (a), the entity does not yet know whether the asset will be leased out in an operating lease rather than sold or leased out under a finance lease and hence may not presume that it will be sold within a year. In (b), whilst in legal form the asset has been sold, it will not be recognised as sold in the financial statements. Sale and leaseback transactions are discussed in Chapter 24 at section 8. As indicated above, the standard contains an exception to the one-year rule. It states that events or circumstances may extend the period to complete the sale beyond one year. Such an extension would not preclude an asset (or disposal group) from being classified as held for sale if the delay is caused by events or circumstances beyond the entity’s control and there is sufficient evidence that the entity remains committed to its plan to sell the asset (or disposal group). This will be the case in the following situations: **[IFRS 5.9, IFRS 5.B1]** (a) At the date an entity commits itself to a plan to sell a non-current asset (or disposal group) it reasonably expects that others (not a buyer) will impose conditions on the transfer of the asset (or disposal group) that will extend the period required to complete the sale, and: (i) Actions necessary to respond to those conditions cannot be initiated until after a firm purchase commitment is obtained. And (ii) A firm purchase commitment is highly probable within one year. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations (b) An entity obtains a firm purchase commitment and, as a result, a buyer or others unexpectedly impose conditions on the transfer of a non-current asset (or disposal group) previously classified as held for sale that will extend the period required to complete the sale, and: (i) Timely actions necessary to respond to the conditions have been taken. And (ii) A favourable resolution of the delaying factors is expected. (c) During the initial one-year period, circumstances arise that were previously considered unlikely and, as a result, a non-current asset (or disposal group) previously classified as held for sale is not sold by the end of that period, and: (i) During the initial one-year period the entity took action necessary to respond to the change in circumstances. (ii) The non-current asset (or disposal group) is being actively marketed at a price that is reasonable, given the change in circumstances. And (iii) The non-current asset (or disposal group) remains available for immediate sale and the sale is highly probable. [IFRS 5 Appendix B]. The standard provides no quantification in relation to the possible length of any extension to the period required to complete a disposal, which means judgement will be required considering individual circumstances. However, as a general observation, the longer the expected extension becomes, as a result of conditions imposed by others, the less likely it is that the sale meets the highly probable threshold. Firm purchase commitment is a defined term in IFRS 5, meaning an agreement with an unrelated party, binding on both parties and usually legally enforceable, that: (1) Specifies all significant terms, including the price and timing of the transactions And (2) Includes a disincentive for non-performance that is sufficiently large to make performance highly probable [IFRS 5 Appendix A] The word ‘binding’ in this definition seems to envisage an agreement still being subject to contingencies. The standard provides an example where a ‘firm purchase commitment’ exists but is subject to regulatory approval (see scenario (a) in Illustration 2-5 below). The standard illustrates each of these exceptions to the one-year rule with the following examples. [IFRS 5.IG Example 5, IFRS 5.IG Example 6, IFRS 5.IG Example 7]. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations Illustration 2-5: Exceptions to the ‘one year rule’ Scenario illustrating (a) above An entity in the power generating industry is committed to a plan to sell a disposal group that represents a significant portion of its regulated operations. Actions necessary to obtain that approval cannot be initiated until after a buyer is known and a firm purchase commitment is obtained. However, a firm purchase commitment is highly probable within one year. In that situation, the conditions for an exception to the one- year requirement would be met. In that situation, the absence of a price reduction demonstrates that the asset is not available for immediate sale. Such disposal may take the form of a sale or a finance lease. [IAS 16.69]. Conversely, IFRS 5 stipulates that a non-current asset (or disposal group) that is to be abandoned must not be classified as held for sale. This includes non-current assets (or disposal groups) that are to be used to the end of their economic life and non-current assets (or disposal groups) that are to be closed rather than sold. The standard explains that this is because its carrying amount will be recovered principally through continuing use. [IFRS 5.13]. 2.1.3 Partial disposals of operations 2.1.3.A Loss of control of a subsidiary The standard provides that when an entity is committed to a sale plan involving loss of control of a subsidiary it must classify all the assets and liabilities of that subsidiary as held for sale when the relevant criteria are met (see section 2.1 above). This is regardless of whether it will retain a non- controlling interest in the former subsidiary after the sale, such as for instance an interest in an associate or a joint venture. [IFRS 5.8A]. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations If the retained interest represents a joint operation, it could be argued that, since the entity retains a direct interest in the underlying assets and obligations for the liabilities after the disposal, the transaction is in substance a sale of parts of the underlying assets and liabilities. On that basis, only the disposed of portions of the assets and liabilities would be classified as held for sale. The counter argument is that the requirement in IFRS 5 does not scope out situations in which the retained interest represents a joint operation. Furthermore, the loss of control of a subsidiary is a significant economic event in that it changes the relationship between the entity and the investee fundamentally, and therefore it would be appropriate to classify all assets and liabilities of the subsidiary as held for sale. Some further argue that classification as held for sale will depend on whether the operation of the joint operation represents a business as defined by IFRS 3 Business Combinations. [IFRS 3 Appendix A]. If it does, it could be argued that applying IFRS 5 better reflects the substance of the transaction, namely that control over a business is lost (i.e., that effectively all assets and liabilities of the subsidiary are being disposed of). On that analysis, classification of all assets and liabilities of the subsidiary as held for sale is appropriate. How we see it Pending any changes to the standard, we believe that entities have an accounting policy choice as to whether to reclassify all assets and liabilities of the subsidiary or only the portion thereof that will not be retained when control is lost in a subsidiary and the retained interest represents a joint operation, to be applied consistently and disclosed. If the subsidiary in question meets the definition of a discontinued operation, the standard’s presentation and disclosure requirements for discontinued operations apply (see section 3.2 below). [IFRS 5.36A]. IFRS 5 does not explicitly extend these requirements to loss of control of a subsidiary in other ways. Given the alignment of the rules on sales with distributions to owners, it seems clear that partial distributions triggering loss of control would result in held for distribution classification. However, control may be lost in other ways than an actual sale or distribution of shares. Such ‘deemed disposals’ are discussed in section 2.1.4 below. 2.1.3.B Partial disposal of an associate or joint venture In accordance with IAS 28 Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures, IFRS 5 will apply to an investment, or a portion of an investment, in an associate or a joint venture that meets the criteria to be classified as held for sale (see section 2.1.2 above). Any retained portion of such an investment that has not been so classified must be accounted for using the equity method until disposal of the portion that is classified as held for sale takes place. After the disposal takes place, any retained interest must be accounted for in accordance with IFRS 9 Financial Instruments unless the retained interest continues to be an associate or a joint venture, in which case the equity method must be used. If such an investment ceases to be classified as held for sale, it must be accounted for using the equity method retrospectively from the date of its original classification as held for sale. Financial statements for the periods since classification as held for sale must be amended accordingly. [IAS 28.20, IAS 28.21]. 2.1.4 Deemed disposals Control may be lost in other ways than an actual sale or distribution of shares. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations 2.2.2 Measurement of non-current assets and disposal groups held for sale or held for distribution to owners 2.2.2.A Measurement on initial classification as held for sale IFRS 5 requires that immediately before the initial classification of an asset (or disposal group) as held for sale, the carrying amount of the asset (or all the assets and liabilities in the group) must be measured in accordance with applicable IFRS accounting standards. [IFRS 5.18]. The same approach is applicable in case of non-current assets (or disposal group) held for distribution to owners. In other words, an entity must apply its usual accounting policies up until the criteria for classification under IFRS 5 are met. Thereafter, a non-current asset (or disposal group) classified as held for sale or for distribution to owners must be measured at the lower of its carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell (distribute). [IFRS 5.15, IFRS 5.15A]. IFRS 13 Fair Value Measurement defines fair value as “the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date” (see Chapter 15). [IFRS 13.9]. Costs to sell are defined as “the incremental costs directly attributable to the disposal of an asset (or disposal group), excluding finance costs and income tax expense.” [IFRS 5 Appendix A]. The same definition applies for costs to distribute. [IFRS 5.15A]. The examples of such costs include broker commissions, legal and title fees necessary to execute the sale, transaction taxes, advisors, levies by regulatory agencies and similar costs. Although the guidance applies equally to both external and internal costs, the internal costs are not often incremental. The costs of holding the assets (i.e., insurance, security services, utility expenses and similar) that are to be incurred between the date of classifying a non-current asset (or disposal group) as held for sale and the date of the disposal are not costs to sell. Those costs are not incremental costs directly attributable to the sale of an asset (or disposal group) as they would be incurred irrespective of the expected disposal. Some costs may be incurred concurrently with the disposal of a non-current asset (or disposal group), but they may not necessarily be the ‘cost to sell’. For example, the sale of a subsidiary may require an entity to pay redundancy costs to employees, however, those costs are not ‘costs to sell’ as they are not essential to the sale itself. The costs to sell should be expensed when they are incurred unless they meet the definition of an asset under other IFRS accounting standards (for example in case of prepayment for future services). When the sale is expected to occur beyond one year, the costs to sell must be measured at their present value. Any increase in the present value of the costs to sell that arises from the passage of time must be presented in profit or loss as a financing cost. [IFRS 5.17]. For disposal groups, the standard adopts a portfolio approach. It requires that if a non-current asset within the scope of its measurement requirements is part of a disposal group, the measurement requirements must apply to the group as a whole, so that the group is measured at the lower of its carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell. [IFRS 5.4]. It will still be necessary to apportion any write down to the underlying assets of the disposal group, but it is not clear whether any element could or should be apportioned to items outside the scope of the standard’s measurement provisions. This is discussed further in section 2.2.3 below. The fair value less costs to sell of the disposal group will take into account fair value of assets and liabilities not recorded on the balance sheet (for instance fair value of unrecognised intangible assets or contingent liabilities) if that is consistent with the assumptions market participants would use when pricing the disposal group. [IFRS 13.11, IFRS 13.22]. If a newly acquired asset (or disposal group) meets the criteria to be classified as held for sale (which, as discussed in section 2.1.2 above, are subtly different for assets acquired exclusively with a view to subsequent disposal), applying the above requirements will result in the asset (or disposal group) being measured on initial recognition at the lower of its carrying amount had it not been so classified (for example, cost) and fair value less costs to sell. This means that if the asset (or disposal group) is acquired as part of a business combination, it will be measured at fair value less costs to sell. [IFRS 5.16]. The treatment of comparatives when the classification as held for sale commences or ceases is discussed in section 4 below. 2.2.5 Changes to a plan of sale or to a plan of distribution 2.2.5.A Assets (or disposal groups) to be retained by the entity An asset (or disposal group) must cease to be classified as held for sale (or distribution) if and when the criteria discussed in section 2.1.2 above are no longer met. [IFRS 5.26]. If an individual asset or liability is removed from a disposal group classified as held for sale or classified as held for distribution, the remaining assets and liabilities of the disposal group must only continue to be measured as a group if the group still meets the criteria to be held for sale (or for distribution) under IFRS 5. Otherwise, the remaining non-current assets of the group that individually meet the criteria must be measured individually at the lower of their carrying amounts and fair values less costs to sell at that date. Any non-current assets that do not meet the criteria must cease to be classified as held for sale or held for distribution. [IFRS 5.29]. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations A non-current asset (or disposal group) that ceases to be classified as held for sale or for distribution (or ceases to be included in a disposal group which is so classified) must be measured at the lower of: (a) Its carrying amount before the asset (or disposal group) was classified as held for sale or for distribution, adjusted for any depreciation, amortisation or revaluations that would have been recognised had the asset (or disposal group) not been so classified And (b) Its recoverable amount at the date of the subsequent decision not to sell or distribute [IFRS 5.27] Regarding (b) above, the standard notes that if the non-current asset is part of a cash-generating unit, its recoverable amount is the carrying amount that would have been recognised after the allocation of any impairment loss arising on that cash-generating unit in accordance with IAS 36. [IFRS 5.27]. Recoverable amount is defined as the higher of: • An asset’s fair value less costs to sell And • Its value in use. Value in use is defined as “the present value of estimated future cash flows expected to arise from the continuing use of an asset and from its disposal at the end of its useful life.” [IFRS 5 Appendix A]. How we see it If a disposal group that ceases to be classified as held for sale (or distribution) contains goodwill that has been previously impaired under IFRS 5, and its value upon reclassification to held for use based on the above requirement exceeds the carrying amount of the group under IFRS 5, we believe the considerations in section 2.2.3 above on the reversal of impairment losses recognised under IFRS 5 also apply. Any required adjustment to the carrying amount of a non-current asset that ceases to be classified as held for sale or for distribution must be included: (a) In profit or loss from continuing operations in the period in which the criteria are no longer met (unless the asset had been revalued in accordance with IAS 16 or IAS 38 before classification as held for sale, in which case the adjustment must be treated as a revaluation increase or decrease) And (b) In the same caption of the statement of comprehensive income used to present any gain or loss recognised in relation to remeasuring non-current assets (or disposal groups) held for sale or distribution but not meeting the definition of a discontinued operation [IFRS 5.28, IFRS 5.37] Financial statements for the periods since classification as held for sale must be amended accordingly if the disposal group or non-current asset that ceases to be classified as held for sale is a subsidiary, joint operation, joint venture, associate, or a portion of an interest in a joint venture or an associate. The adjustment must be presented in the same caption in the statement of comprehensive income described at (b) above. The treatment of comparative information on the cessation of held-for-sale classification is discussed in section 4.2 below. 2.2.5.B Change in method of disposal An entity may change the manner in which an asset (or disposal group) will be disposed of from being held for sale to being held for distribution to owners (or vice versa). Such a change raises the question as to whether the previous accounting treatment under IFRS 5 must be ‘unwound’ and started afresh based on the new disposal method, or whether a seamless transition from one to the other must be treated as a continuation of one overall disposal plan. When the manner of disposal changes directly from one method to the other, the change in classification is considered to be a continuation of the original plan of disposal. In such cases: (a) The guidance discussed in section 2.2.5.A above does not apply. Rather, the classification, presentation and measurement requirements that are applicable to the new method of disposal must be applied (see sections 2.1 and 2.2 above); that is, without first treating the asset (or disposal group) as ceasing to be held for sale by reversing the preceding accounting treatment. (b) Any reduction or increase in the fair value less costs to sell/costs to distribute of the non-current asset (or disposal group) must be recognised as discussed in section 2.2.3 above. And (c) The date of classification as held for sale or for distribution does not change; nor does such a change preclude an extension of the ‘one year rule’ (both as discussed in section 2.1.2 above if the relevant conditions are met). [IFRS 5.26A]. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations Discontinued operations As discussed in section 3.2 below, IFRS 5 requires the presentation of a single amount on the face of the or statement of comprehensive income relating to discontinued operations, with further analysis either on the face of the statement or in the notes. 3.1 Definition of a discontinued operation IFRS 5 defines a discontinued operation as: [IFRS 5.32, IFRS 5 Appendix A] Extract from IFRS 5 Appendix A Defined terms discontinued operation A component of an entity that either has been disposed of or is classified as held for sale and: (a) represents a separate major line of business or geographical area of operations, (b) is part of a single co-ordinated plan to dispose of a separate major line of business or geographical area of operations or (c) is a subsidiary acquired exclusively with a view to resale. Classification as held for sale is discussed in section 2.1 above. For the purposes of the above definition, a ‘component of an entity’ is also defined by the standard as comprising “operations and cash flows that can be clearly distinguished, operationally and for financial reporting purposes, from the rest of the entity. In other words, a component of an entity will have been a cash-generating unit or a group of cash-generating units while being held for use.” [IFRS 5.31, IFRS 5 Appendix A]. IFRS 5 defines cash generating unit in the same way as IAS 36, that is as “the smallest identifiable group of assets that generates cash inflows that are largely independent of the cash inflows from other assets or groups of assets.” [IFRS 5 Appendix A]. Cash generating units are discussed in Chapter 21 at section 3. How we see it In our view, it seems unlikely that this definition of a component would ever be met by a single non- current asset (for example, a single building or single equity-accounted investment). Accordingly, a discontinued operation will also be one or more ‘disposal groups’ which is a group of assets to be disposed of, by sale or otherwise, together as a group in a single transaction, and liabilities directly associated with those assets that will be transferred in the transaction (discussed in section 2.1.1 above). Furthermore, even if a single equity-accounted investment was considered to meet the definition of a component, only in rare circumstances would it represent a separate major line of business or geographical area of operations. [IFRS 5.32(a)]. Considering that the definition of discontinued operations also requires that it represents a “separate major line of business or geographical area of operations” (emphasis added), a question may arise whether the adjective ‘major’ refers also to the latter component of the definition, i.e., whether also a geographical operation needs to be ‘major’ for the definition of discontinued operations to be met. How we see it We believe that the term ‘major’ in the definition of the discontinued operation refers to both ‘line of business’ and ‘geographical area of operations‘, i.e., a discontinued operations needs to represent either a major line of business or a major geographical area of operations. That is consistent with the intention of the Board that the size of the unit that could be classified as discontinued is not too small and therefore the information provided by separately presenting discontinued operations is useful. [IFRS 5.BC69]. Consequently, a component which neither represents a major line of business nor a major geographical area of operations would not meet the definition of a discontinued operation and cannot be presented as such. Non-current assets held for sale and discontinued operations The meaning of ‘separate major line of business or geographical area’ was raised with the IFRS IC. It concluded, in January 2016, that it must be considered for a broad-scope project on IFRS 5 (possible future developments of IFRS 5 are discussed in section 6 below).7 Pending more guidance, determining whether a line of business or geographical area is major may involve judgement and require appropriate disclosures in accordance with IAS 1 (specifically IAS 1.122). The concept of ‘single co-ordinated plan’ is not defined in the standard but suggests that the component representing a major line of business or a major geographical area need not be sold in one transaction in order to be presented as a discontinued operation. For instance, it may be that the entity is expecting to sell the component piecemeal to two or more buyers, and that the piecemeal transactions are expected to occur at different times. In such a case, the different parts of the component may not qualify for held for sale classification at the same time, for instance because one or more parts are not expected to be sold within one year. The requirements for presenting discontinued operations would, however, still be applicable for the part (or parts) expected to be sold within one year in the current period, because the corresponding piecemeal disposal is a part of a single co-ordinated plan to sell a major line of business or geographical area of operations. The other part (or parts) would be presented under the discontinued operations guidance in the future periods when they meet the held for sale classification requirements. In these cases, the different parts of a component may not need to represent major lines of business or major geographical areas by themselves. As discussed in section 2.1.2.C above, IFRS 5 stipulates that a non-current asset (or disposal group) that is to be abandoned must not be classified as held for sale. This includes non-current assets (or disposal groups) that are to be used to the end of their economic life and non-current assets (or disposal groups) that are to be closed rather than sold. However, if the disposal group to be abandoned meets the criteria above, the standard requires it to be treated as a discontinued operation ‘at the date on which it ceases to be used’. [IFRS 5.13]. In other words, the treatment as discontinued only starts in the period when abandonment actually occurs (see Illustration 3-1 and Illustration 3-2 below). A non-current asset that has been temporarily taken out of use must not be accounted for as if it had been abandoned. [IFRS 5.14]. Accordingly, it would not be presented as a discontinued operation. An example given by the standard is of a manufacturing plant that ceases to be used because demand for its product has declined but which is maintained in workable condition and is expected to be brought back into use if demand picks up. The plant is not regarded as abandoned. [IFRS 5.IG Example 8]. However, in these circumstances an impairment test and review of useful life will need to be performed in accordance with IAS 36 (discussed in Chapter 21) and, as appropriate, IAS 16 or IAS 38 (discussed in Chapter 19 and # Chapter 18, respectively). The standard provides an example of a discontinued operation arising from abandonment upon which the following Illustration is based. [IFRS 5.IG Example 9]. All work stops at the cotton mills during the year ended 31 December 20X2. [IFRS 5.33(b)]. If the required analysis is presented on the face of the statement of comprehensive income it must be presented in a section identified as relating to discontinued operations, i.e., separately from continuing operations. [IFRS 5.33A]. The standard also makes clear that any gain or loss on the remeasurement of a non- current asset (or disposal group) classified as held for sale that does not meet the definition of a discontinued operation must not be included within these amounts for discontinued operations, but be included in profit or loss from continuing operations. [IFRS 5.37]. Only those expenses that will no longer be incurred by the entity after the sale of the operations can be attributed to discontinued operations. Typically, most group overheads do not cease with the disposal and will therefore be allocated entirely to continuing operations. That means any transactions between discontinued and continuing operations are eliminated as usual in the consolidation. As a consequence, the amounts ascribed to the continuing and discontinued operations will be income and expense only from transactions with counterparties external to the group. However, the presentation of the eliminations is not addressed by the standard. If all intragroup transactions between continuing and discontinued operations are eliminated without any adjustments, the results presented on the face of the statement of comprehensive income will not represent the activities of the operations as individual entities. Some might consider the results for the continuing and discontinued operations on this basis to be of little use to readers of accounts. An argument could be made that allocating external transactions to or from the discontinued operation would yield more meaningful information. Entities will need to consider whether any (and, if so, what) changes are necessary to comparative information as previously reported whenever: • Non-current assets or disposal groups first become classified as such And • That classification ceases 4.1 Treatment of comparative information on initial classification as held for sale 4.1.1 The statement of comprehensive income For non-current assets and disposal groups not qualifying as discontinued operations there are no special requirements relating to presentation in the statement of comprehensive income, accordingly no restatement of comparative amounts would be relevant. [IFRS 5.34]. Accordingly, adjustments to the comparative information as originally reported will be necessary for those disposal groups categorised as discontinued operations. 4.1.2 The statement of financial position IFRS 5 states that an entity shall not reclassify or re-present amounts presented for non-current assets or for the assets and liabilities of disposal groups classified as held for sale in the statements of financial position for prior periods to reflect the classification in the statement of financial position for the latest period presented. [IFRS 5.40]. Typically, this would require a ‘catch-up’ depreciation charge as depreciation would not have been accounted for while it was held for sale. The standard explicitly requires this to be a current year charge. [IFRS 5.28]. This indicates that for non-current assets and disposal groups ceasing to be so classified the measurement of items in comparative information (statement of comprehensive income and statement of financial position) must not be revisited. This requirement applies equally to discontinued operations. The entity shall present that adjustment in the same caption in the statement of comprehensive income” within continuing operations used to record any gains and losses on non-current assets (or disposal groups) held for sale. [IFRS 5.28, IFRS 5.37]. IAS 28 clarifies that, as regards associates and joint ventures, the amendment of financial statements ‘for the periods since classification as held for sale’ means retrospectively from the date of its original classification as held for sale. [IAS 28.21]. This clarification is not repeated in IFRS 10 or IFRS 11 Joint Arrangements. However, we believe the clarification also applies to assets or disposal groups within the scope of those standards and that this is the most common approach seen in practice. As a result, when a disposal group or non-current asset that was classified as held for sale represented an entire subsidiary, joint operation, joint venture or associate or was a portion of an interest in a joint venture or associate, and subsequently no longer qualifies as held for sale, financial statements must be amended retrospectively as though the disposal group or non- current asset never qualified as held for sale. **中文:** 正如其名称所示，该准则涉及两个相互区别但存在重叠的广泛领域。第一个领域涉及当非流动资产主要通过处置而非持续使用收回时，其计量与列报。相关要求尤其包括： • 划分为持有待售类别（以及出售计划的变更） • 停止计提折旧和摊销 • 将待处置资产及资产与负债组减记至其账面价值与公允价值减去出售费用后的较低者 以及 • 在财务状况表中单独列示此类资产与负债

**中文:** 第二个事项涉及终止经营的概念。这方面的要求涵盖：识别终止经营并在损益中列报其经营成果、重述比较信息以反映该经营在当期期末的状态，以及与该经营相关的现金流量信息。该准则规定了上述事项的披露要求。

**中文:** 需要了解的内容 • IFRS 5 规定了资产及资产与负债组划分为持有待售类别的标准、终止经营的定义，以及相应的计量、列报和披露要求。 • 该准则的设计通常要求基于主体适用的具体事实和情况做出重大判断。 • 多年来，关于该准则的若干事项被提出，并由国际财务报告准则解释委员会（IFRS IC）讨论，随后提交至国际会计准则理事会（IASB），例如处置组中减值损失的分配，或持续经营与终止经营之间集团内部交易的处理。迄今为止，IASB 尚未对 IFRS 5 进行实施后审查，也未处理这些事项。

- **中文:** • 主体需审慎考虑披露评估终止经营及非流动资产（或处置组）处置财务影响所需的信息，包括基于主体具体事实和情况作出的重大判断及会计政策选择的基础与影响。 持有待售的非流动资产与终止经营 IFRS 5的目标与范围 IFRS 5的目标是规范持有待售资产的会计处理，以及终止经营的列报与披露。具体而言，该准则要求符合其分类为持有待售标准的非流动资产（以及“处置组”中的相关负债与流动资产，详见下文第2.1.1节）应： (a) 按账面价值与公允价值减去出售成本后的较低者计量，并停止对其计提折旧（或摊销）；且 (b) 在财务状况表中单独列示，终止经营的结果在综合收益表中单独列报 [IFRS 5.1]。 分类与列报要求适用于所有已确认的非流动资产与处置组，但准则的计量规定存在若干例外情况 [IFRS 5.2, IFRS 5.5]。这些问题将在下文第2.2节进一步讨论。 IFRS 5中适用于分类为持有待售资产（或处置组）的分类、列报与计量要求，同样适用于分类为向所有者（以其所有者身份）进行分配的资产 [IFRS 5.5A]。此内容将在下文第2.1.2节讨论。

**中文:** 持有待售的非流动资产与终止经营 持有待售或拟分配给所有者的非流动资产（及处置组）

**中文:** 2.1 持有待售或拟分配给所有者的非流动资产（及处置组）的分类 《国际财务报告准则第5号》频繁提及流动资产与非流动资产，并对两者定义如下：[IFRS 5附录A]

**中文:** 摘自IFRS 5附录A：定义术语 流动资产 企业应将资产归类为流动资产的情形包括： (a) 预计在正常经营周期内实现该资产，或意图出售或耗用该资产； (b) 持有该资产的主要目的是用于交易； (c) 预计在报告期后十二个月内实现该资产；或 (d) 该资产为现金或现金等价物（定义见《国际会计准则第7号》），除非该资产在报告期后至少十二个月内被限制用于交换或清偿负债。

**中文:** 非流动资产 不符合流动资产定义的资产。

**中文:** 处置组 拟通过出售或其他方式一并处置的一组资产，以及与该等资产直接相关且将在交易中转移的负债。若该组为已按照《国际会计准则第36号——资产减值》（2004年修订）第80-87段要求分配商誉的现金产出单元，或为该类现金产出单元内的经营业务，则该组包括企业合并中取得的商誉。

**中文:** 前两项定义与《国际会计准则第1号——财务报表列报》（见第3章第3.1.1节讨论）中的定义相同。

**中文:** 2.1.1 处置组的概念 如其标题所示，IFRS 5规定了持有待售非流动资产的会计处理方法，即账面金额主要通过出售而非持续使用收回的资产。[IFRS 5.6]

**中文:** 然而，该准则同样适用于构成“处置组”组成部分的某些负债和流动资产。准则指出，企业有时会通过单笔交易一并处置一组资产，可能附带部分直接相关的负债。[IFRS 5.4] 常见示例为处置子公司。针对此类情形，IFRS 5引入了处置组的概念，将其定义为通过出售或其他方式在单笔交易中作为整体一并处置的资产组，以及交易中转让的与该等资产直接相关的负债。若该组为已根据《国际会计准则第36号——资产减值》（见第21章讨论）要求分配商誉的现金产出单元，或为该类现金产出单元内的经营业务，则该组包含企业合并中取得的商誉。[IFRS 5附录A] “在单笔交易中一并”的表述表明，仅购买方承担的负债可纳入该组。因此，企业需用销售收益偿还的任何借款应排除在处置组之外。持有待售的非流动资产和终止经营 准则进一步说明，处置组：• 可能由一组现金产出单元、单个现金产出单元或现金产出单元的一部分构成。当资产或资产组的现金流量预计主要来源于出售而非持续使用时，其对其他资产现金流量的依赖程度降低，原属现金产出单元一部分的处置组将成为一个独立的现金产出单元。且 • 可能包含企业的任何资产和负债，包括流动资产、流动负债以及不属于IFRS 5计量要求范围的资产（见下文第2.2节）。

**中文:** [IFRS 5.4] 终止经营将在下文第3节讨论。如前所述，单项非流动资产几乎不可能满足终止经营的定义。因此，终止经营通常包含一个或多个处置组，而处置组往往不符合终止经营的定义。此外，终止经营涉及可明确区分的现金流量以及主要业务或主要地理区域，但处置组无需具备这些特征。2.1.1.A 持有待售处置组中的递延所得税资产和负债 与处置组内项目相关的可能记录有各种递延所得税资产和负债，例如处置组中单项资产和负债的计税基础与账面金额之间暂时性差异产生的递延所得税，或未使用税务亏损产生的递延所得税资产。根据处置组的定义，企业仅将拟转移给买方的资产和负债归类为持有待售。[IFRS 5 附录A] 该准则未就递延所得税资产和负债是否或何时应纳入持有待售处置组提供进一步指引。我们的观点 我们认为，若递延所得税资产和负债将由买方承担（接管），则将其纳入处置组符合处置组的定义。然而，若预期处置将导致与处置组中保留的资产和负债相关的暂时性差异转回，则相关递延所得税资产和负债应由卖方保留（并最终结算），因此必须从处置组中剔除。

**中文:** 尽管任何评估都必须反映预期处置交易的具体事实和情况以及所涉特定司法管辖区的税收法规，但如果处置组是一个独立的法律实体且处置将通过"股权交易"（即出售该实体股份）的方式进行，递延所得税通常将由买方承担。在此情况下，许多司法管辖区中，被出售法律实体中单项资产和负债的计税基础（有时称为"税务历史"）将得以保留，并随之转移给买方。因此，这些递延所得税资产和负债应纳入处置组。[IFRS 5附录A，IAS 12.51A]。还需注意确保与被出售实体相关的任何"外部"暂时性差异（即卖方在法律实体中的投资账面金额与其投资计税基础之间的暂时性差异所产生的递延所得税）不纳入处置组，因为这属于卖方的负债或资产。"外部"暂时性差异在第29章第7.6节中讨论。如果出售的是单项资产和负债的组合（即处置将通过"资产交易"方式进行），税务机关通常会将此类出售视为相关资产的收回（或相关负债的清偿），并导致卖方先前暂时性差异的转回。买方对这些资产和负债的新计税基础通常基于购买价格确定。卖方将在处置时结算（实现）递延所得税，因此不应将这些递延所得税余额纳入处置组。[IFRS 5附录A，IAS 12.51A]。预期处置的形式（"股权交易"或"资产交易"）通常被视为出售计划的重要组成部分。

**中文:** 如下文第2.1.2.B节所述，为使销售极可能发生，计划发生重大变更的可能性应很小。[IFRS 5.8] 因此，若主体难以确定预期销售将以股份交易还是资产交易方式进行，则可能表明尚未满足持有待售标准。根据具体事实和情况，处置形式可能对确定哪些资产和负债应归类为持有待售具有相关性，如下文示例2-1所示。持有待售的非流动资产和终止经营 示例2-1：持有待售投资性房地产重新计量产生的递延所得税负债 经营房地产业的主体A承诺执行一项计划，出售其全资子公司持有的一处购物中心。该子公司的唯一资产为投资性房地产本身、贸易应收款项及现金。子公司除因投资性房地产账面金额与其计税基础（等于购物中心建设直接相关成本）之间的差额产生的递延所得税负债外，无其他负债。根据处置计划采用股份交易还是资产交易形式，处置组的范围将受到影响，如下方案例1和案例2所示。 1. 主体A管理层拟通过股份交易方式处置该投资性房地产。该处置组满足所有可归类为持有待售的标准（见下文第2.1.2节）。在主体A经营所在的司法管辖区，出售股份不征收税款。递延所得税负债将与子公司所有其他资产一并转移给买方。因此，主体将子公司的递延所得税负债纳入持有待售处置组。

**中文:** 主体A的管理层拟通过资产交易方式处置该投资性房地产。在处置时，该子公司将终止确认该投资性房地产，并实现与该投资性房地产相关的递延所得税负债。因此，该递延所得税负债被排除在处置组之外。上述两种情形对处置组的范围产生重大影响（假设该子公司的递延所得税负债具有重要性），因此在判断持有待售分类是否适当前，需审慎考虑通过股权交易或资产交易实现处置的意图。仅凭意图通常不足以认定处置形式变更的可能性较低。处置产生的任何所得税影响均依据《国际会计准则第12号——所得税》进行会计处理，且如以下第2.2.2.A节所述，在计量持有待售的处置组时不计入销售成本。拟处置一组资产或子公司时，在拟议交易导致相关资产和负债根据《国际财务报告准则第5号》重新分类至处置组之前，可能影响递延所得税资产和负债的计量。例如，由于预期收回方式为出售而非持续使用，可能需要重新评估递延所得税资产和负债的计量。[《国际会计准则第12号》第51段] 相关内容在第29章第7.5.8节（涉及递延所得税资产的确认）和第8.4.11节（涉及资产收回或负债结算的预期方式）中讨论。代表终止经营的处置组在处置时产生的任何所得税利得或损失，应在终止经营项目中列报（见下文第3.2节）。

**中文:** 2.1.2 划分为持有待售或向所有者进行分配 IFRS 5要求，若非流动资产（或处置组）的账面价值主要通过出售交易而非持续使用收回，则应将其划分为持有待售。[IFRS 5.6] 为此目的，出售交易包括根据《国际会计准则第16号——不动产、厂场和设备》（见第19章第4.4节讨论）具有商业实质的非流动资产交换其他非流动资产的情形。[IFRS 5.10] 对于按流动性列报分类的资产（见第3章第3.1.1节），非流动资产指预计在报告日后超过十二个月收回金额的资产。[IFRS 5.2] 确定资产何时（以及是否）不再主要通过持续使用收回，而转为主要通过出售收回，是关键的区分点，该准则的大部分内容均用于解释如何进行此项判断。对于资产（或处置组）划分为持有待售，需满足以下条件： (a) 该资产（或处置组）在其当前状态下仅需满足此类资产（或处置组）出售的通常及惯常条款，即可立即出售； (b) 其出售极可能发生；[IFRS 5.7] (c) 预计该处置将在分类之日起一年内符合确认为已完成出售的条件。[IFRS 5.8] 以下将进一步讨论这些标准。 持有待售的非流动资产与终止经营 若资产（或处置组）已划分为持有待售，但不再满足上述标准，则主体应终止将其划分为持有待售。[IFRS 5.26] 处置计划的变更将在下文第2.2.5节讨论。从持有待用重新分类为持有待售，源于相关事实和情况的变化。

**中文:** 此外，即使单项权益法核算的投资被认为符合组成部分的定义，也仅在极少数情况下才能代表独立的主要业务线或主要经营地区。[IFRS 5.32(a)]。考虑到终止经营的定义还要求其代表“独立的主要业务线或经营地区”（强调为原文所加），可能产生一个问题：形容词“主要”是否也适用于定义中的后一部分，即经营地区是否也需要是“主要”的才能满足终止经营的定义。我们的观点 我们认为，终止经营定义中的“主要”一词同时适用于“业务线”和“经营地区”，即终止经营必须代表主要业务线或主要经营地区。这与理事会的意图一致，即能够被归类为终止经营的单元规模不应过小，从而单独列报终止经营所提供的信息具有有用性。[IFRS 5.BC69]。因此，既不代表主要业务线也不代表主要经营地区的组成部分，不符合终止经营的定义，不能作为终止经营列报。持有待售的非流动资产和终止经营 “独立的主要业务线或经营地区”的含义曾提交至IFRS解释委员会。该委员会于2016年1月得出结论，认为需将其纳入IFRS 5的广泛范围项目（IFRS 5可能的未来发展将在下文第6节讨论）。7 在获得更多指引之前，判断业务线或经营地区是否为主要可能涉及判断，并需要根据《国际会计准则第1号》（特别是IAS 1.122）进行适当披露。

**中文:** “单一协调计划”这一概念在准则中并未明确定义，但其含义表明：构成主要业务线或主要地理区域的组成部分，无需通过单次交易出售即可被列为终止经营。例如，企业可能计划将该组成部分分拆出售给两个或多个买方，且这些分拆交易预计在不同时间发生。在此情况下，该组成部分的不同部分可能无法同时满足持有待售分类条件——例如，其中某部分（或多部分）预计无法在一年内出售。然而，终止经营的列报要求仍适用于当前期间预计在一年内出售的部分（或多部分），因为相应的分拆处置属于出售主要业务线或主要经营地理区域的单一协调计划的一部分。其余部分（或多部分）将在未来期间满足持有待售分类条件时，按照终止经营指引进行列报。在这些情形下，组成部分的不同部分本身可能无需构成主要业务线或主要地理区域。如上述第2.1.2.C节所述，《国际财务报告准则第5号》规定，拟废弃的非流动资产（或处置组）不得归类为持有待售，包括将使用至经济寿命结束的非流动资产（或处置组），以及拟关闭而非出售的非流动资产（或处置组）。但若拟废弃的处置组符合上述标准，准则要求将其“自停止使用之日起”作为终止经营处理。[IFRS 5.13]

**中文:** 3.3 持续经营与终止经营之间的交易

**中文:** 尽管存在上述单行列报，终止经营在集团财务报表中仍予以合并。这意味着终止经营与持续经营之间的任何交易均按常规在合并中予以抵销。因此，归属于持续经营和终止经营的金额仅来自与集团外部对手方进行的交易。然而，准则并未涉及抵销的列报方式。若持续经营与终止经营之间的所有集团内部交易均未经任何调整而直接抵销，则综合收益表正面上列报的结果将无法反映各经营业务作为独立个体的活动。

**中文:** 有人认为，基于此基础列报的持续经营和终止经营结果，对财务报表使用者而言价值有限。也有观点认为，将外部交易分配至终止经营或从终止经营中剥离，能够提供更具意义的信息。国际财务报告准则解释委员会（IFRS IC）对此进行了讨论，并于2016年1月发布了议程决议。8 《IFRIC动态》2016年1月刊，可查阅国际财务报告准则基金会网站。持有待售的非流动资产和终止经营。在该决议中，IFRS IC指出：“解释委员会注意到，IFRS 5和IAS 1均未包含可凌驾于IFRS 10《合并财务报表》合并要求之上的终止经营列报规定。解释委员会同时指出，IFRS 10第B86(c)段要求抵销的不仅包括集团内部利润，还包括集团内部交易相关的收入和费用。因此，解释委员会认为，未抵销集团内部交易将不符合IFRS 10的抵销要求。”IFRS IC进一步指出：“解释委员会还注意到，IFRS 5第30段要求主体列报和披露信息，以使财务报表使用者能够评估终止经营和处置活动的财务影响。基于此目标，解释委员会认为，根据具体事实和情况，主体可能需要提供额外披露，以便使用者评估终止经营的财务影响。” 我们的观点 我们认为，当持续经营与终止经营之间的交易金额重大时，是否需要补充信息，以及如需补充，其形式和内容应如何确定，显然属于需要专业判断的事项。

**中文:** 通常有两种替代方法来消除持续经营与终止经营之间的交易：一是直接消除所有交易而不作任何调整；二是消除集团内部交易，但进行调整以反映持续经营与终止经营之间的交易在未来如何体现在持续经营中。在某些情况下，后一种方法可被视为提供更相关的结果。以下示例基于IFRS IC工作人员文件（随附于IFRS IC9讨论）中的说明性案例，可对此加以说明：

**中文:** 流动资产 企业应当将资产归类为流动资产，当满足以下条件时： (a) 预计在正常经营周期内变现、出售或消耗该资产； (b) 持有该资产的主要目的是为了交易； (c) 预计在报告期后十二个月内变现该资产；或 (d) 该资产为现金或现金等价物（定义见《国际会计准则第7号》），除非该资产在报告期后至少十二个月内被限制用于交换或清偿负债。
