deals with IAS 33 Earnings per Share. This accounting standard requires certain entities to disclose earnings per share figures in their financial statements. It also sets out how those figures are to be computed. IAS 33 requires disclosure of both basic and diluted earnings per share. Where relevant, earnings per share is required separately for continuing operations, for discontinued operations and in total. Basic and diluted earnings per share are intended to be measures of the interest each of the parent’s ordinary shares has in the performance of the entity. Diluted earnings per share also gives effect to dilutive potential ordinary shares outstanding during the reporting period, for example convertible loans, convertible preference shares, share options and warrants, including those arising from share-based payment arrangements. Earnings used in determining basic earnings per share are an entity’s profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders. This can differ from an entity’s profit or loss, for example, it will not include profit or loss attributable to non-controlling interests and certain adjustments will be necessary in respect of dividends and other payments in respect of preference shares classified as equity. Further adjustments may be required in the calculation of diluted earnings per share. The standard also sets out requirements for the number of shares to be used in the calculation which will often be different for basic and for diluted earnings per share. Entities may disclose earnings per share based on other components of comprehensive income, although the number of shares used must be the same as specified by IAS 33 and additional disclosures are required to explain the earnings figure used. Once IFRS 18 Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements is applied certain restrictions on the presentation of additional per share figures are introduced. In particular: any such amounts must be presented only in the notes; and, the numerator used must be either a line item in the primary statement or a management-defined performance measure. What you need to know • IAS 33 applies only to entities with publicly traded ordinary shares (or those in the process of becoming so) and to other entities choosing to present earnings per share. • The standard sets out requirements for the number of shares to be used in each computation. • Certain adjustments are required to earnings for the purposes of diluted earnings per share. • IAS 33 requires disclosure of basic and diluted earnings per share. • Certain changes come into force once IFRS 18 is applied. Earnings per share Introduction Earnings per share (EPS) is one of the most widely quoted statistics in financial analysis. It came into great prominence in the US during the late 1950s and early 1960s due to the widespread use of the price earnings ratio (PE) as a yardstick for investment decisions. As a result, standard setters in some jurisdictions (notably the US and the UK) have had rules on EPS for many years. However, it was not until 1997 that an international accounting standard on the subject was published. IAS 33 was introduced for accounting periods beginning on or after 1 January 1998. In December 2003, as part of the improvements project, the IASB updated IAS 33 to provide more detailed guidance in some complex areas. There have been no substantive changes since. The requirements of IAS 33 are discussed in sections 2 to 7 below. The standard includes illustrative examples of particular issues and one comprehensive worked example. The illustrative examples are included where relevant in the text of the chapter, whilst the comprehensive worked example is included as an appendix. 1.1 Definitions IAS 33 defines a number of its terms and these are dealt with in the text of this chapter where appropriate. One term which is particularly pervasive is ‘fair value’. This term is defined and explained in IFRS 13 Fair Value Measurement and is discussed in Chapter 15. [IAS 33.8]. However, in the context of share-based payments the term fair value has the meaning used in IFRS 2 Share-based Payment. The relevance of share-based payment to EPS is discussed in section 6.4.5 below; IFRS 2 is discussed in Chapter 30. [IAS 33.47A]. Objective and scope of IAS 33 2.1 Objective IAS 33 sets out its objective as follows: [IAS 33.1] Extract from IAS 33 Objective The objective of this Standard is to prescribe principles for the determination and presentation of earnings per share, so as to improve performance comparisons between different entities in the same reporting period and between different reporting periods for the same entity. Even though earnings per share data have limitations because of the different accounting policies that may be used for determining ‘earnings’, a consistently determined denominator enhances financial reporting. The focus of this Standard is on the denominator of the earnings per share calculation. The standard requires the computation of both basic and diluted EPS, explaining the objective of each as follows: • The objective of basic earnings per share information is to provide a measure of the interests of each ordinary share of a parent entity in the performance of the entity over the reporting period. [IAS 33.11]. And • The objective of diluted earnings per share is consistent with that of basic earnings per share - to provide a measure of the interest of each ordinary share in the performance of an entity - while giving effect to all dilutive potential ordinary shares outstanding during the period. [IAS 33.32]. The underlying logic here is that EPS, including diluted EPS, is to be an historical performance measure. This impacts particularly on the reporting of diluted EPS, in steering it away from an alternative purpose: to warn of potential future dilution. Indeed, the tension between these differing objectives is evident in the standard. As discussed more fully in section 6.4.6 below, IAS 33 sets out a very restrictive regime for including certain potentially dilutive shares in the diluted EPS calculation. Yet diluted EPS is only to take account of those potential shares that would dilute earnings from continuing operations (see section 6.3.1 below) which seems to have more of a forward looking ‘warning signal’ flavour. Discontinued operations are discussed in Chapter 5. Earnings per share 2.2 Scope IAS 33 applies to: (a) The separate or individual financial statements of an entity: (i) Whose ordinary shares or potential ordinary shares are traded in a public market (a domestic or foreign stock exchange or an over-the-counter market, including local and regional markets) Or (ii) That files, or is in the process of filing, its financial statements with a securities commission or other regulatory information for the purpose of issuing ordinary shares in a public market And (b) The consolidated financial statements of a group with a parent: (i) Whose ordinary shares or potential ordinary shares are traded in a public market (a domestic or foreign stock exchange or an over-the-counter market, including local and regional markets) Or (ii) That files, or is in the process of filing, its financial statements with a securities commission or other regulatory information for the purpose of issuing ordinary shares in a public market [IAS 33.2] IAS 33 also applies to any other entity that discloses earnings per share. [IAS 33.3]. Where both the parent’s consolidated and separate financial statements are presented, the standard only requires consolidated earnings per share to be given. If the parent chooses to present EPS data based on its separate financial statements, the standard requires that the disclosures be restricted to the face of the parent-only statement of comprehensive income (or separate income statement) and not be included in the consolidated financial statements. [IAS 33.4]. In January 2014, the IASB published IFRS 14 Regulatory Deferral Accounts. This standard allows a first- time adopter within its scope to continue to account for regulatory deferral account balances in its first IFRS financial statements in accordance with its previous GAAP when it adopts IFRS. The standard introduces limited changes to some previous GAAP accounting practices for regulatory deferral account balances, which are primarily related to the presentation of these accounts. For entities applying IFRS 14 certain additional EPS disclosures are required. This is discussed in Chapter 6 at section 5.22.6.B. Earnings per share The basic EPS IAS 33 requires the computation of basic EPS for the profit or loss (and, if presented, the profit or loss from continuing operations) attributable to ordinary equity holders. [IAS 33.9]. It defines, or rather describes, basic earnings per share in the following manner: [IAS 33.10] Extract from IAS 33 Basic earnings per share shall be calculated by dividing profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent entity (the numerator) by the weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding (the denominator) during the period. 3.1 Earnings The starting point for determining the earnings figure to be used in the basic EPS calculation (both for total earnings and, if appropriate, earnings from continuing operations) is the net profit or loss for the period attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent entity. [IAS 33.12]. This will, in accordance with IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements (and, once IFRS 18 is applied, at the latest for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, in accordance with that standard) include all items of income and expense, including, dividends on preference shares classified as liabilities and tax and is stated after the deduction of non- controlling interests. [IAS 33.13, IAS 33.A1]. This is then adjusted for the after-tax amounts of preference dividends, differences arising on the settlement of preference shares, and other similar effects of preference shares classified as equity. [IAS 33.12]. These adjustments are discussed in section 5.2 below. 3.2 Number of shares An ordinary share is defined as “an equity instrument that is subordinate to all other classes of equity instruments”. [IAS 33.5]. Equity instrument has the same meaning as in IAS 32 Financial Instruments: Presentation that is “any contract that evidences a residual interest in the assets of an entity after deducting all of its liabilities”. [IAS 33.8, IAS 32.11]. IAS 33 goes on to observe that ordinary shares participate in profit for the period only after other types of share such as preference shares have participated. [IAS 33.6]. The standard also clarifies that there may be more than one class of ordinary share and requires the computation and presentation of EPS for each class that has a different right to share in profit for the period. [IAS 33.6, IAS 33.66]. In practice, it is usually straightforward to determine which instruments are ordinary shares for EPS purposes. The treatment of different classes of shares is discussed in section 5.4 below. The basic rule in IAS 33 is that all outstanding ordinary shares are brought into the basic EPS computation - time-weighted for changes in the period (changes in ordinary shares are discussed in section 4 below). [IAS 33.19]. There are three exceptions to this: • Ordinary shares that are issued as partly paid are included in the weighted average as a fraction of a share based on their dividend participation relative to fully paid shares (so if although only partly paid, they ranked equally for dividends they would be included in full). [IAS 33.A15]. • Treasury shares, which are presented in the financial statements as a deduction from equity, are not considered outstanding for EPS purposes for the period they are held in treasury. Although not stated explicitly in the standard itself, this requirement is clearly logical (as although the shares are still legally in issue, they are accounted for as if redeemed) and is illustrated in one of the examples appended to the standard (see Illustration 4-1 in section 4.1 below). [IAS 33.IE2]. And • Shares that are contingently returnable (that is, subject to recall) are not treated as outstanding until they cease to be subject to recall, and hence are excluded from basic EPS until that time. [IAS 33.24]. The standard contains some specific guidance on when newly issued ordinary shares are to be considered outstanding. In general, shares are to be included from the date consideration is receivable (considered by the standard generally to be the date of their issue), for example: [IAS 33.21] • Shares issued in exchange for cash are included when cash is receivable • Shares issued on the voluntary reinvestment of dividends on ordinary or preference shares are included when the dividends are reinvested • Shares issued as a result of the conversion of a debt instrument to ordinary shares are included as of the date interest ceases accruing • Shares issued in place of interest or principal on other financial instruments are included as of the date interest ceases accruing Earnings per share • Shares issued in exchange for the settlement of a liability of the entity are included as of the settlement date • Shares issued as consideration for the acquisition of an asset other than cash are included as of the date on which the acquisition is recognised • Shares issued in exchange for the rendering of services to the entity are included as the services are rendered • Shares that will be issued upon the conversion of a mandatorily convertible instrument are included in the calculation of basic earnings per share from the date the contract is entered into [IAS 33.23] Most of these provisions are straightforward, however some are worthy of note. Shares issued in exchange for services will be accounted for in accordance with IFRS 2, with a charge to income matched by a credit to equity. IAS 33 has some guidance on the inclusion of such potential shares in diluted EPS (see section 6.4.5 below); however, there is no further elaboration of the meaning of ‘included as the services are rendered’. What seems to be implicit in the phrase is that the shares concerned vest unconditionally as services are rendered. On that basis, clearly it would be appropriate to include shares in basic EPS as entitlement to them vests, notwithstanding that the actual issue of shares may be at a different time. However, a very common form of share-based remuneration involves entitlement to shares vesting at the end of an extended period conditional on future events (typically continued employment and sometimes specific future performance). Such arrangements are clearly conditionally issuable shares and therefore, would be excluded from basic EPS until vesting. Indeed, when discussing employee share schemes in the context of diluted EPS the standard is explicit, as follows. [IAS 33.48]. Extract from IAS 33 Employee share options with fixed or determinable terms and non-vested ordinary shares are treated as options in the calculation of diluted earnings per share, even though they may be contingent on vesting. They are treated as outstanding on the grant date. Performance-based employee share options are treated as contingently issuable shares because their issue is contingent upon satisfying specified conditions in addition to the passage of time. Contingently issuable shares are discussed in section 6.4.6 below. In respect of the final bullet point, the standard does not define what a mandatorily convertible instrument is. One view would be that the requirement to account for the shares in EPS from inception must mean it refers to instruments where the proceeds also are received at inception. On that basis, it would exclude a forward contract for the issue of shares which (as required by the first bullet above) would increase the denominator of basic EPS only from the time the cash is receivable. Similarly, in the reverse situation of a forward contract to redeem ordinary shares the shares would only be removed from basic EPS when the consideration becomes payable. Another view would be that all binding agreements to issue or redeem ordinary shares should be reflected in basic EPS when the entity becomes party to the arrangement. A further possible complexity is the question of whether or not a symmetrical treatment for the issue and redemption of shares should be applied for EPS purposes. Whilst that certainly seems logical, it is not beyond question in all circumstances particularly given the asymmetrical accounting treatment for certain derivatives over own shares required by IAS 32 (discussed in Chapter 43 at section 5). More generally, the standard goes on to say the timing of inclusion is determined by the attaching terms and conditions, and also that due consideration must be given to the substance of any contract associated with the issue. [IAS 33.21]. Ordinary shares that are issuable on the satisfaction of certain conditions (contingently issuable shares) are to be included in the calculation of basic EPS only from the date when all necessary conditions have been satisfied; in effect when they are no longer contingent. [IAS 33.24]. This provision is interpreted strictly, as illustrated in Example 7 appended to the standard (see Illustration 6-7 in section 6.4.6.A below). In that example, earnings in a year, by meeting certain thresholds, would trigger the issue of shares. Because it is not certain that the condition is met until the last day of the year (when earnings become known with certainty) the new shares are excluded from basic EPS until the following year. Where shares will be issued at some future date (that is, solely after the passage of time) they are not considered contingently issuable by IAS 33, as the passage of time is a certainty. [IAS 33.24]. In principle, this would seem to mean that they should be included in basic EPS from the agreement date. However, careful consideration of the individual facts and circumstances would be necessary. The calculation of the basic EPS is often simple but a number of complications can arise; these may be considered under the following two headings: (a) Changes in ordinary shares outstanding (b) Matters affecting the numerator These are discussed in the next two sections. Earnings per share Changes in outstanding ordinary shares Changes in ordinary shares outstanding can occur under a variety of circumstances, the most common of which are dealt with below. Whenever such a change occurs during the accounting period, an adjustment is required to the number of shares in the EPS calculation for that period; furthermore, in certain situations the EPS for previous periods will also have to be recalculated. 4.1 Weighted average number of shares Implicit in the methodology of IAS 33 is a perceived correlation between the capital of an entity (or rather the income-generating assets it reflects) and earnings. Accordingly, to compute EPS as a performance measure requires adjusting the number of shares in the denominator to reflect any variations in the period to the capital available to generate that period’s earnings. The standard observes that using the weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period reflects the possibility that the amount of shareholders’ capital varied during the period as a result of a larger or smaller number of shares being outstanding at any time. The weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding during the period is the number of ordinary shares outstanding at the beginning of the period, adjusted by the number of ordinary shares bought back or issued during the period multiplied by a time-weighting factor. The time-weighting factor is the number of days that the shares are outstanding as a proportion of the total number of days in the period; IAS 33 notes that a reasonable approximation of the weighted average is adequate in many circumstances. [IAS 33.20]. Computation of a weighted average number of shares is shown in the following Illustration: [IAS 33.IE2] Illustration 4-1: Calculation of weighted average number of shares Shares issued Treasury shares* Shares outstanding ## January 20X2 Balance at beginning of year 2,000 1,700 ## May 20X2 Issue of new shares for cash - 2,500 ## December 20X2 Purchase of treasury shares for cash - 2,250 ## December 20X2 Balance at year end 2,800 2,250 Calculation of weighted average: (1,700 × 5/12) + (2,500 × 6/12) + (2,250 × 1/12) = 2,146 shares Or (1,700 × 12/12) + (800 × 7/12) - (250 × 1/12) = 2,146 shares * Treasury shares are equity instruments reacquired and held by the issuing entity itself or by its subsidiaries. The use of a weighted average number of shares is necessary because the increase in the share capital would have affected earnings only for that portion of the year during which the issue proceeds were available to management for use in the business. 4.2 Purchase and redemption of own shares An entity may, if it is authorised to do so by its constitution and it complies with any relevant legislation, purchase or otherwise redeem its own shares. Assuming this is done at fair value, then the earnings will be apportioned over the weighted average share capital in issue for the year. This was shown in Illustration 4-1 in section 4.1 above in relation to the purchase of treasury shares. If, on the other hand, the repurchase is at significantly more than market value, then IAS 33 requires adjustments to be made to EPS for periods before buy-back. This is discussed in section 4.3.5 below. 4.3 Changes in ordinary shares without corresponding changes in resources IAS 33 requires the number of shares used in the calculation to be adjusted (for all periods presented) for any transaction (other than the conversion of potential ordinary shares) that changes the number of shares outstanding without a corresponding change in resources. [IAS 33.26]. This is also to apply when some, but not all, such changes have happened after the year-end but before the approval of the financial statements. Earnings per share The standard gives the following as examples of changes in the number of ordinary shares without a corresponding change in resources: (a) A capitalisation or bonus issue (sometimes referred to as a stock dividend) (b) A bonus element in any other issue, for example a bonus element in a rights issue to existing shareholders (c) A share split (d) A reverse share split (share consolidation) [IAS 33.27] Another example not mentioned by the standard would be any bonus element in a buy-back, such as a put warrant involving the repurchase of shares at significantly more than their fair value. The adjustments required to EPS for each of these is discussed below. As noted above, IAS 33 requires retrospective adjustment for all such events that happen in the reporting period. However, it only requires restatement for those in (a), (c) and (d) if they happen after the year-end but before the financial statements are authorised for issue. [IAS 33.64]. 4.3.1 Capitalisation, bonus issue, share split and share consolidation 4.3.1.A Capitalisation, bonus issues and share splits A capitalisation or bonus issue or share split has the effect of increasing the number of shares in issue without any inflow of resources, as further ordinary shares are issued to existing shareholders for no consideration. Consequently, no additional earnings will be expected to accrue as a result of the issue. The additional shares are treated as having been in issue for the whole period and also included in the EPS calculation of all earlier periods presented so as to give a comparable result. For example, on a two-for-one bonus issue, the number of ordinary shares outstanding before the issue is multiplied by three to obtain the new total number of ordinary shares, or by two to obtain the number of additional ordinary shares. [IAS 33.28]. The EPS calculation involving a bonus issue is shown in the following Illustration. [IAS 33.IE3]. Illustration 4-2: A bonus issue Profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent entity 20X1 CU180 Profit attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent entity 20X2 CU600 Ordinary shares outstanding until 30 September 20X2 Bonus issue 1 October 20X2 2 ordinary shares for each ordinary share outstanding at 30 September 20X2 200 × 2 = Basic earnings per share 20X2 CU600 (200 + 400) = CU1.00 Basic earnings per share 20X1 CU180 (200 + 400) = CU0.30 Because the bonus issue was without consideration, it is treated as if it had occurred before the beginning of 20X1, the earliest period presented. 4.3.1.B Stock dividends Stock or scrip dividends refer to the case where an entity offers its shareholders the choice of receiving further fully paid up shares in the company as an alternative to receiving a cash dividend. It could be argued that the dividend foregone represents payment for the shares, usually at fair value, and hence no restatement is appropriate. Alternatively, the shares could be viewed as being, in substance, bonus issues which require the EPS for the earlier period to be adjusted. IAS 33 seems to suggest the latter view, as it notes that capitalisation or bonus issues are sometimes referred to as stock dividends. However, entities often refer to these arrangements as dividend reinvestment plans which suggests the acquisition of new shares for valuable consideration. Earnings per share This distinction is a factual one. If an entity (say, through proposal and subsequent approval by shareholders) has a legal obligation to pay a dividend in cash or, at the shareholder’s option, shares, then the cash payment avoided, if the stock dividend is taken up, is consideration for the shares. This may be equivalent to an issue at fair value or it may contain some bonus element requiring retrospective adjustment of EPS. In practice, the fair value of shares received as a stock dividend alternative may exceed the cash alternative; this is often referred to as an enhanced stock dividend. In these cases, IAS 33 requires a bonus element to be identified, and prior EPS figures restated accordingly. This is essentially the same as adjustments for the bonus element in a rights issue, discussed in section 4.3.3 below. Furthermore, in this scenario, during the period between the obligation coming into existence and its settlement (in cash or shares) it could be argued to represent a written call option and hence potentially affect diluted EPS (see section 6.4.2.B below). How we see it Given that the standard is silent on this aspect of some stock dividends we do not believe that such an approach was intended. In any event, we generally do not believe the effect on diluted EPS would be significant. Conversely, if the entity issues new shares instead of a dividend it would be a bonus issue requiring full retrospective adjustment to EPS. 4.3.1.C Share consolidations Occasionally, entities will consolidate their equity share capital into a smaller number of shares. Such a consolidation generally reduces the number of shares outstanding without a corresponding outflow of resources, and this would require an adjustment to the denominator for periods before the consolidation. [IAS 33.29]. 4.3.2 Share consolidation with a special dividend Share consolidations as discussed in section 4.3.1.C above normally do not involve any outflow of funds from the entity. However, entities may return surplus cash to their shareholders by paying special dividends accompanied by a share consolidation, the purpose of which is to maintain the value of each share following the payment of the dividend. This issue is specifically addressed by IAS 33. The normal rule of restating the outstanding number of shares for all periods for a share consolidation is not applied when the overall effect is a share repurchase at fair value because in such cases, the reduction of shares is the result of a corresponding reduction in resources. In such cases, the weighted average number of shares is adjusted for the consolidation from the date the special dividend is recognised. [IAS 33.29]. 4.3.3 Rights issue A rights issue is a popular method through which entities are able to access the capital markets for further capital. Under the terms of such an issue, existing shareholders are given the opportunity to acquire further shares in the entity on a pro rata basis to their existing shareholdings. The ‘rights’ shares will usually be offered either at the current market price or at a price below that. In the former case, the treatment of the issue for EPS purposes is as discussed in section 4.1 above. However, where the rights price is at a discount to market it is not quite as straightforward, since the issue is equivalent to a bonus issue (see section 4.3.1 above) combined with an issue at full market price. In such cases, IAS 33 requires an adjustment to the number of shares outstanding before the rights issue to reflect the bonus element inherent in it. [IAS 33.26, IAS 33.27]. The bonus element of the rights issue available to all existing shareholders is given by the following adjustment factor, sometimes referred to as the bonus fraction: [IAS 33.A2] Fair value per share immediately before the exercise of rights Theoretical ex-rights fair value per share The fair value per share immediately before the exercise of rights is the actual price at which the shares are quoted inclusive of the right to take up the future shares under the rights issue. Where the rights are to be traded separately from the shares the fair value used is the closing price on the last day on which the shares are traded inclusive of the right. [IAS 33.A2]. The ‘ex-rights fair value’ is the theoretical price at which the shares would be expected to be quoted, other stock market factors apart, after the rights issue shares have been issued. It is calculated by adding the aggregate fair value of the shares immediately before the exercise of the rights to the proceeds from the exercise, and dividing by the number of shares outstanding after the exercise. [IAS 33.A2]. The EPS calculation involving a rights issue is shown in the following Illustration. [IAS 33.IE4]. During the period that the rights are outstanding they represent, strictly speaking, a written call option over the entity’s shares which could have implications for diluted EPS (see section 6.4.2 below). It is possible that shares could be issued as a result of open offers, placings and other offerings of equity shares not made to existing shareholders, at a discount to the market price. In such cases, it would be necessary to consider whether the issue contained a bonus element, or rather simply reflected differing views on the fair value of the shares. The latter is a more realistic alternative. Accordingly, the shares would be dealt with on a weighted average basis without calculating any bonus element when computing the EPS. Earnings per share 4.3.4 B share schemes One method by which some entities have returned capital to shareholders is the so-called ‘B share scheme’. These schemes involve issuing ‘B shares’ (usually undated preference shares with low or zero coupons) to existing shareholders, either as a bonus issue or via a share split, following which the ordinary shares are consolidated. The ‘B shares’ are then repurchased or redeemed for cash and cancelled. The overall effect is intended to be the same as a repurchase of ordinary shares at fair value, and accordingly no retrospective adjustment to EPS is necessary, assuming that the intention is achieved. [IAS 33.29]. 4.3.5 Put warrants priced above market value As noted in section 4.3 above, an example of a change in the number of shares outstanding without a corresponding change in resources not mentioned by the standard would be any bonus element in a buy- back, such as a put warrant involving the repurchase of shares at significantly more than their fair value. The accounting requirements for such instruments are discussed in Chapter 43 at section 5. IAS 33 does not give an illustrative calculation for a put warrant at significantly more than fair value, but it does for the more familiar rights issue (which are discussed in section 4.3.3 above). In a rights issue, new shares are issued at a discount to market value, whereas with put warrants shares are bought back at a premium to market value. In both cases, the remaining shares are viewed as being devalued for the purposes of comparing EPS over time. Applying the requirement to adjust EPS when there is a change in the number of shares without a corresponding change in resources would require that put warrants are treated as a reverse rights issue. This would mean calculating a similar ‘adjustment factor’, and applying it to the number of shares outstanding before the transaction. The difference in the calculation would be that the number of shares issued and the consideration received for them would be replaced by negative amounts representing the number of shares put back to the entity and the amount paid for them. Entities often enter into commitments to issue shares in the future which would result in a change in basic EPS. IAS 33 refers to such commitments as potential ordinary shares, which it defines as “a financial instrument or other contract that may entitle its holder to ordinary shares”. [IAS 33.5]. Examples of potential ordinary shares given by IAS 33 are: (a) Financial liabilities or equity instruments, including preference shares, that are convertible into ordinary shares. (b) Options and warrants (whether accounted for under IAS 32 or IFRS 2). (c) Shares that would be issued upon the satisfaction of conditions resulting from contractual arrangements, such as the purchase of a business or other assets. [IAS 33.7]. When potential shares are actually issued, the impact on basic EPS will be two-fold. First, the number of shares in issue will change; second, profits could be affected, for example by lower interest charges or the return made on cash inflows. Scenarios whereby such an adjustment to basic EPS is unfavourable are described by the standard as dilution, defined as “a reduction in earnings per share or an increase in loss per share resulting from the assumption that convertible instruments are converted, that options or warrants are exercised, or that ordinary shares are issued upon the satisfaction of specified conditions”. [IAS 33.5]. This potential fall in EPS is quantified by computing diluted EPS, and as a result: (a) Profit or loss attributable to equity holders is increased by the after-tax amount of dividends and interest recognised in the period in respect of the dilutive potential ordinary shares and is adjusted for any other changes in income or expense that would result from the conversion of the dilutive potential ordinary shares. And (b) The weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding is increased by the weighted average number of additional ordinary shares that would have been outstanding assuming the conversion of all dilutive potential ordinary shares. [IAS 33.32]. 6.2 Calculation of diluted EPS IAS 33 requires a diluted EPS figure to be calculated for the profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent and, if presented, profit or loss from continuing operations attributable to them. [IAS 33.30]. For these purposes, the profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders and the weighted average number of shares outstanding is adjusted for the effects of all potential ordinary shares. [IAS 33.31]. In calculating diluted EPS, the number of shares to be used is that used in calculating basic EPS, plus the weighted average number of shares that would be issued on the conversion of all the dilutive potential ordinary shares into ordinary shares. As is the case for outstanding shares in the basic EPS calculation, potential ordinary shares are to be weighted for the period they are outstanding. [IAS 33.36, IAS 33.38]. Accordingly, potential ordinary shares: • Are deemed to have been converted into ordinary shares at the beginning of the period or, if not in existence at the beginning of the period, the date of their issue [IAS 33.36] • Which are cancelled or allowed to lapse are included only for the period they are outstanding And • Which convert into ordinary shares during the period are included up until the date of conversion (from which point they will be included in the basic EPS) [IAS 33.38] The number of dilutive potential ordinary shares is to be determined independently for each period presented, and not subsequently revisited. In particular, prior periods’ EPS are not restated for changes in assumptions about the conversion of potential shares into shares. IAS 33 also stresses that the number of dilutive potential ordinary shares included in the year-to-date period is not a weighted average of the dilutive potential ordinary shares included in each interim computation. [IAS 33.37, IAS 33.65]. This is illustrated in the comprehensive illustrative example accompanying IAS 33, the substance of which is reproduced in the appendix to this chapter. In our view, the requirements of the standard in (b) and (c) above relating to settlement options are somewhat confused. In particular, they seem to envisage a binary accounting model based on the strict legal form of settlement (cash or shares). However, IAS 32 sets out rules for three different settlement methods - net cash, net shares and gross physical settlement (discussed in Chapter 43 at section 5). One consequence of the above is that, if taken literally, the numerator is only required to be adjusted to remove items of income or expense arising from a liability when there is a choice of settlement method. However, mandatory net share settlement also gives rise to a liability and income/expense under IAS 32. In our view, any such income statement items need to be removed for diluted EPS purposes. Potential ordinary shares are ‘antidilutive’ when their conversion to ordinary shares would increase earnings per share or decrease loss per share. [IAS 33.5, IAS 33.43]. The calculation of diluted earnings per share must not assume conversion, exercise, or other issue of potential ordinary shares that would have an antidilutive effect on earnings per share. [IAS 33.5]. However, it was largely written before the significant developments in accounting for such instruments (IFRS 2 and IAS 32). As a result, individual facts and circumstances must be considered and judgement is required in some circumstances to address the dilutive effects on EPS. Earnings per share IAS 33 clearly states that “Options and warrants … do not affect the numerator of the calculation” [IAS 33.44] and this text was added in 2003 as part of the improvements project, so clearly drafted against the back drop of the impending move to expensing share-based payments and also the (then) recent changes to IAS 32 regarding accounting for derivatives over an entity’s own shares. As regards employee share options in particular, neither IAS 33 (as updated by IFRS 2) nor the worked example appended to it (see Illustration 6-6 in section 6.4.5 below) make reference to removing either some or all the charge when computing diluted EPS. [IAS 33.A7]. (c) Where option proceeds are required to be applied to redeem debt or other instruments of the entity (see section 6.4.2.F below). [IAS 33.A9]. The unpaid balance is assumed to represent proceeds used to purchase ordinary shares. The number of shares included in diluted earnings per share is the difference between the number of shares subscribed and the number of shares assumed to be purchased. [IAS 33.A16]. The mechanics of this treatment are not further spelt out in the standard, but the phrase ‘treated as a fraction of an ordinary share’ is not repeated. Instead, it is ‘the number of shares subscribed’ which the standard says is to be compared to the number assumed purchased to measure dilution. However, ‘the number of shares subscribed’ is not defined. Whilst this could be read to mean that the remaining unpaid consideration is to be treated as the exercise price for options over all of the shares issued in partly paid form, we believe the better interpretation is that the unpaid capital be viewed as the exercise price for options over the proportion of the shares not reflected in the basic EPS. This would mean that if the average share price for the period were the same as the total issue price, then no dilution would be reported. Furthermore, an issue of partly paid shares, say 50% paid with 50% dividend entitlement is economically identical to an issue of half the quantity as fully paid (with full dividend entitlement) and a forward contract for the remaining half. In that scenario, the issued shares would be incorporated into the basic and diluted EPS in full from the date of issue. The forward contract would be included in diluted EPS calculation by comparing the contracted number of shares with the number of shares that could be bought out of proceeds based on the average share price for the period. Contingently issuable ordinary shares are defined as “ordinary shares issuable for little or no cash or other consideration upon satisfaction of specified conditions in a contingent share agreement.” A contingent share agreement is defined by the standard as “an agreement to issue shares that is dependent on the satisfaction of specified conditions.” [IAS 33.5]. The basic rule is that the number of contingently issuable shares to be included in the diluted EPS calculation is “based on the number of shares that would be issuable if the end of the period were the end of the contingency period”. [IAS 33.52]. This requirement to look at the status of the contingency at the end of the reporting period, rather than to consider the most likely outcome, seems to have the overall result of reducing the amount of dilution disclosed. The discussions in the standard cover three broad categories: earnings-based contingencies, share price- based contingencies, and other contingencies. These are discussed in turn below. The number of shares contingently issuable may depend on future earnings and future prices of the ordinary shares. In such cases, the standard makes clear that the number of shares included in the diluted EPS calculation is based on both conditions (i.e., earnings to date and the current market price at the end of the reporting period). In other words, contingently issuable shares are not included in the diluted EPS calculation unless both conditions are met. [IAS 33.55]. 6.4.6.A Earnings-based contingencies The standard discusses the scenario where shares would be issued contingent upon the attainment or maintenance of a specified amount of earnings for a period. In that case, the calculation of diluted earnings per share is based on the number of ordinary shares that would be issued if the amount of earnings at the end of the reporting period were the amount of earnings at the end of the contingency period. … Earnings per share As a result, earnings-based contingencies need to be viewed as an absolute cumulative hurdle which either is met or not met at the reporting date. Often, such contingencies may be contractually expressed in terms of annual performance over a number of years, say an average of CU1 million profit per year for three years. In such a scenario, ‘the attainment or maintenance of a specified amount of earnings for a period’ would mean generating a total of CU3 million of profits. If that is achieved by the end of a reporting period, the shares are outstanding for diluted EPS purposes and included in the computation if the effect is dilutive. It could, perhaps, be argued that the potential shares should be considered outstanding if profits of CU1 million were generated at the end of the first year. However, the requirement that the calculation be ‘based on the number of ordinary shares that would be issued if the amount of earnings at the end of the reporting period were the amount of earnings at the end of the contingency period’ means that the test must be: would shares be issued if the current earnings of CU1 million were all the profits earned by the end of the three-year contingency period? In this example, the answer is no, as that amount of earnings would fall short of averaging CU1 million per year. The standard then notes that, because earnings may change in a future period, the calculation of basic EPS does not include such contingently issuable shares until the end of the contingency period because not all necessary conditions have been satisfied. [IAS 33.53]. The example does illustrate that the earnings target is a cumulative hurdle over the entire contingency period (four reporting periods in the example) rather than including potential shares based on the assumption that the level of quarterly profit would be maintained for the four quarters. The standard only discusses earnings criteria based on absolute measures; in the example above a cumulative profit in excess of CU2,000,000. In our experience such criteria are rare. In practice, criteria are often phrased in terms of relative performance against an external benchmark. Examples would be earnings growth targets of inflation plus 2% or EPS growth being in the top quartile of a group of competitors. For contingencies such as these it is impossible to establish an absolute target in order to ask whether it is met at the period end. For example, consider the earnings contingency in IAS 33, discussed above, to achieve profits in excess of CU2,000,000 over four quarters. If this instead required the profits to be CU2,000,000 adjusted in line with inflation, it would be impossible to know how many shares would be issued if the cumulative profit at the end of the second quarter of CU2,300,000 were the amount of earnings at the end of the contingency period. Until the end of the year the absolute level of profit required would be unknown; it would be more or less than CU2,000,000 depending on the level of inflation or deflation over the period. There would seem to be (at least) two different ways of interpreting the requirements of IAS 33 in such a scenario, each resulting in a different diluted EPS figure. One approach would be to consider such criteria as being based on ‘a condition other than earnings or market price’. That would mean (as discussed in section 6.4.6.C below) that the number of shares brought into diluted EPS would be based on the status of the condition at the end of the reporting period. [IAS 33.56]. If the target was earnings for the year in excess of CU2,000,000 adjusted in line with inflation and at the end of the second quarter inflation had been 4%, then the target would become CU2,080,000 and hence 220,000 shares would be included for diluted EPS for the second quarter. An alternative approach would be to regard it as an earnings-based contingency and make an assumption as to future inflation over the contingency period. This would allow a cumulative hurdle to be calculated and compared with actual earnings to date. If at the end of the second quarter it was estimated that the annual inflation for the year was 5%, then the target would become CU2,100,000 and hence 200,000 shares would be included for diluted EPS for the second quarter. Given the lack of clarity in the standard, it seems likely that either of the above approaches may be selected in practice. Earnings per share 6.4.6.B Share price-based contingencies The provisions here are more straightforward. In these cases, if the effect is dilutive, the calculation of diluted EPS is based on the number of shares that would be issued if the market price at the end of the reporting period were the market price at the end of the contingency period. If the condition is based on an average of market prices over a period of time that extends beyond the end of the reporting period, the average for the period of time that has lapsed is used. Again the standard explains that, because the market price may change in a future period, the calculation of basic earnings per share does not include such contingently issuable ordinary shares until the end of the contingency period because not all necessary conditions have been satisfied. [IAS 33.54]. 6.4.6.C Other contingencies The requirement regarding contingencies not driven by earnings or share price is as follows: “assuming that the present status of the condition remains unchanged until the end of the contingency period, the contingently issuable ordinary shares are included in the calculation of diluted earnings per share according to the status at the end of the reporting period.” [IAS 33.56]. The standard illustrates the ‘other contingency’ rules by the example of shares being issued depending upon the opening of a specified number of retail sites, and such a contingency is included in the numerical example in the standard (see Illustration 6-7 in section 6.4.6.A above). As is the case for earnings-based contingencies discussed above, it would seem that such conditions are always deemed to be expressed as a cumulative hurdle which may or may not be met by the end of the reporting period. Accordingly, the required treatment would be the same if the condition had been expressed in terms of achieving a certain average annual level of shop openings. 6.4.7 Potential ordinary shares of investees A subsidiary, joint venture or associate may issue to parties other than the parent or investors with joint control of, or significant influence over the investee potential ordinary shares that are convertible into either ordinary shares of the subsidiary, joint venture or associate, or ordinary shares of the parent or investors with joint control of, or significant influence over the investee (the reporting entity). If these potential ordinary shares of the subsidiary, joint venture or associate have a dilutive effect on the basic EPS of the reporting entity, they are included in the calculation of diluted earnings per share. [IAS 33.40]. The standard requires that such potential ordinary shares be included in the calculation of diluted EPS as follows: (a) Instruments issued by a subsidiary, joint venture or associate that enable their holders to obtain ordinary shares of the subsidiary, joint venture or associate are to be included in calculating the diluted EPS data of the subsidiary, joint venture or associate. Those EPS are then included in the reporting entity’s EPS calculations based on the reporting entity’s holding of the instruments of the subsidiary, joint venture or associate. (b) Instruments of a subsidiary, joint venture or associate that are convertible into the reporting entity’s ordinary shares are to be considered among the potential ordinary shares of the reporting entity for the purpose of calculating diluted EPS. Similarly, options or warrants issued by a subsidiary, joint venture or associate to purchase ordinary shares of the reporting entity are considered among the potential ordinary shares of the reporting entity in the calculation of consolidated diluted EPS. **[IAS 33.A11].** For the purpose of determining the EPS effect of instruments issued by a reporting entity that are convertible into ordinary shares of a subsidiary, joint venture or associate, the standard requires that the instruments are assumed to be converted and the numerator (profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent entity) adjusted as necessary in accordance with the normal rules (see section 6.2.1 above). In addition to those adjustments, the numerator is adjusted for any change in the profit or loss recorded by the reporting entity (such as dividend income or equity method income) that is attributable to the increase in the number of ordinary shares of the subsidiary, joint venture or associate outstanding as a result of the assumed conversion. The denominator of the diluted EPS calculation is not affected because the number of ordinary shares of the reporting entity outstanding would not change upon assumed conversion. [IAS 33.A12]. Presentation, restatement and disclosure 7.1 Presentation As discussed in Chapter 3 at section 3.2.1, IAS 1 requires (and at the latest for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, IFRS 18 requires - see chapter 4 at section 1.2) that all items of income and expense be presented either: (a) In a single statement of profit or loss and comprehensive income Or (b) In two separate statements: (i) A statement of profit or loss And (ii) A statement, beginning with profit or loss, presenting items of other comprehensive income **[IAS 1.10A]** If the approach in (b) is followed, the separate statement of profit or loss must be displayed immediately before the statement of comprehensive income. [IAS 1.10A]. If (a) is adopted, the EPS presentational requirements below apply to that single statement. If (b) is chosen, the requirements apply to the separate statement of profit or loss only and not the separate statement of comprehensive income. [IAS 33.4A, IAS 33.67A, IAS 33.68A]. IAS 33 requires the presentation of basic and diluted EPS (with equal prominence and even if the amounts are negative - i.e., a loss per share) for each period for which a statement of comprehensive income (or separate income statement) is presented. [IAS 33.66, IAS 33.69]. This is required for the profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders for: (a) Overall profit (b) Profit or loss from continuing operations And (c) Profit or loss from discontinued operations, if any [IAS 33.66, IAS 33.68] In the case of (a) and (b), separate figures are required for each class of ordinary shares with a different right to share in profits for the period. The figures for (a) and (b) must be displayed on the face of the statement. [IAS 33.66]. Those for (c) may be either on the face or in the notes. [IAS 33.68]. The standard states that if diluted EPS is given for at least one period it must be given for all periods presented. IAS 33 notes that if basic and diluted EPS are equal, dual presentation can be accomplished in one line in the statement. [IAS 33.67]. Regarding (c), the wording of the standard is not very clear. In particular, if an entity has more than one discontinued operation it does not specify whether separate EPS disclosures are required for each or whether one aggregate figure is needed. The wording leans to the former, as it uses the singular - “An entity that reports a discontinued operation shall disclose the basic and diluted amounts per share for the discontinued operation …”. However, IFRS 5 Non-current Assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations only requires the statement of comprehensive income (or separate income statement) to identify the total result from all discontinued operations. [IFRS 5.33]. The presentation of EPS data in addition to that required by IAS 33 is discussed in section 5.5 above. Earnings per share 7.2 Restatement IAS 33 contains requirements to restate prior periods’ EPS for events that change the number of shares outstanding without a corresponding change in resources. Additionally, it specifies circumstances when EPS is not to be restated. Basic and diluted EPS for all periods presented is to be adjusted for: • Events (other than the conversion of potential ordinary shares) which change the number of ordinary shares without a corresponding change in resources (discussed in section 4.3 above) [IAS 33.26, **IAS 33.64]** • The effects of errors and adjustments resulting from changes in accounting policies accounted for retrospectively (see section 5.3 above) [IAS 33.64] • The effects of group reorganisations that are accounted for as a pooling of interests (discussed in section 4.6 above) No adjustment is to be made: • To basic or diluted EPS when a share consolidation is combined with a special dividend where the overall commercial effect is that of a share repurchase at fair value (discussed in section 4.3.2 above) **[IAS 33.29]** • To previously reported diluted EPS due to changes in the prices of ordinary shares which would have given a different dilutive effect for options and warrants [IAS 33.47] • To prior period diluted EPS as a result of a contingency period coming to an end without the conditions attaching to contingently issuable shares being met [IAS 33.52] Or • To prior period diluted EPS for changes in the assumptions used in the calculations or for the conversion of potential ordinary shares into ordinary shares [IAS 33.65] 7.3 Disclosure IAS 33 requires disclosure of the following: (a) The amounts used as the numerators in calculating basic and diluted EPS, and a reconciliation of those amounts to profit or loss attributable to the parent entity for the period. The reconciliation must include the individual effect of each class of instruments that affects EPS. (b) The weighted average number of ordinary shares used as the denominator in calculating basic and diluted earnings per share, and a reconciliation of these denominators to each other. The reconciliation must include the individual effect of each class of instruments that affects EPS. (c) Instruments (including contingently issuable shares) that could potentially dilute basic EPS in the future, but were not included in the calculation because they were antidilutive for the period(s) presented. (d) A description of ordinary share transactions or potential ordinary share transactions (other than those accounted for in EPS for the year - see section 4.3 above - in which case that fact is to be stated), that occur after the end of the reporting period and that would have changed significantly the number of ordinary shares or potential ordinary shares outstanding at the end of the period if those transactions had occurred before the end of the reporting period. [IAS 33.70]. Examples of transactions in (d) include: (a) An issue of shares for cash (b) An issue of shares when the proceeds are used to repay debt or preference shares outstanding at the end of the reporting period (c) The redemption of ordinary shares outstanding (d) The conversion or exercise of potential ordinary shares outstanding at the end of the reporting period into ordinary shares (e) An issue of options, warrants, or convertible instruments (f) The achievement of conditions that would result in the issue of contingently issuable shares Earnings per share The standard observes that EPS amounts are not adjusted for such transactions occurring after the reporting period because such transactions do not affect the amount of capital used to produce profit or loss for the period. [IAS 33.71]. Changes in ordinary shares are discussed in section 4 above. The standard observes that financial instruments and other contracts generating potential ordinary shares may incorporate terms and conditions that affect the measurement of basic and diluted earnings per share. These terms and conditions may determine whether any potential ordinary shares are dilutive and, if so, the effect on the weighted average number of shares outstanding and any consequent adjustments to profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders. ## 每股收益 概述 本章涉及IAS 33《每股收益》。

**中文:** 本会计准则要求特定主体在其财务报表中披露每股收益数据，并规定了这些数据的计算方法。国际会计准则第33号要求同时披露基本每股收益和稀释每股收益。在相关情况下，需分别列示持续经营业务、终止经营业务及合计的每股收益。基本每股收益和稀释每股收益旨在衡量母公司每股普通股在主体业绩中的权益份额。稀释每股收益还需考虑报告期内已发行的潜在稀释性普通股的影响，例如可转换贷款、可转换优先股、股票期权及认股权证（包括源自股份支付安排的此类工具）。计算基本每股收益时使用的收益，是指归属于普通股权益持有人的主体损益。该数值可能与主体损益存在差异，例如不包含归属于非控制性权益的损益，且需针对归类为权益的优先股股利及其他支付进行特定调整。在计算稀释每股收益时，可能还需进一步调整。该准则同时规定了计算中应使用的股份数量要求，基本每股收益与稀释每股收益的股份数量通常不同。主体可基于其他综合收益组成部分披露每股收益，但使用的股份数量必须符合国际会计准则第33号的规定，且需额外披露以说明所采用的收益数据。一旦采用《国际财务报告准则第18号——财务报表列报与披露》，将对额外每股收益数据的列报施加特定限制。

**中文:** 具体而言：此类金额必须仅在附注中列报；且所使用的分子必须是主要报表中的单列项目或管理层定义的业绩指标。需知要点 • 《国际会计准则第33号》仅适用于拥有公开交易普通股（或正在申请公开交易）的主体，以及其他选择列报每股收益的主体。 • 该准则规定了各项计算中应使用的股份数量要求。 • 计算稀释每股收益时需对收益进行特定调整。 • 《国际会计准则第33号》要求披露基本每股收益和稀释每股收益。 • 应用《国际财务报告准则第18号》后，某些变更将生效。该变更适用于自2027年1月1日或之后开始的期间，允许提前采用。 每股收益 引言 每股收益是财务分析中引用最广泛的统计指标之一。20世纪50年代末至60年代初，由于市盈率被广泛用作投资决策的衡量标准，该指标在美国备受重视。因此，部分司法管辖区（尤其是美国和英国）的准则制定机构多年来一直对每股收益制定规则。然而，直到1997年才发布了关于该主题的国际会计准则。《国际会计准则第33号》自1998年1月1日或之后开始的会计期间生效。2003年12月，作为改进项目的一部分，国际会计准则理事会更新了《国际会计准则第33号》，为某些复杂领域提供了更详细的指引。此后未发生实质性变更。下文第2至第7节将讨论《国际会计准则第33号》的要求。该准则包含特定问题的示例说明及一个综合性工作示例。示例说明已纳入本章相关正文，综合性工作示例则作为附录列示。

**中文:** 1.1 定义 《国际会计准则第33号》对其多项术语进行了定义，并在本章正文中酌情予以说明。其中一项尤为普遍的术语是"公允价值"。该术语在《国际财务报告准则第13号——公允价值计量》中定义并解释，并在第15章中讨论。[IAS 33.8] 然而，在股份支付的背景下，"公允价值"一词具有《国际财务报告准则第2号——股份支付》中所使用的含义。股份支付与每股收益的相关性将在下文第6.4.5节讨论；《国际财务报告准则第2号》则在第30章中讨论。[IAS 33.47A]

**中文:** 《国际会计准则第33号》的目标与范围 2.1 目标 《国际会计准则第33号》将其目标规定如下：[IAS 33.1] 摘自《国际会计准则第33号》目标 本准则的目标是规定每股收益的确定和列报原则，以改进同一报告期内不同主体之间以及同一主体不同报告期之间的业绩比较。尽管由于确定"收益"时可能采用不同的会计政策，每股收益数据存在局限性，但一致确定的计算分母能够增强财务报告的可比性。本准则的重点在于每股收益计算的分母。

**中文:** 该准则要求计算基本每股收益和稀释每股收益，并分别说明其目标如下： • 基本每股收益信息的目标是提供衡量母公司每一普通股在报告期内主体业绩中所占权益的指标。[IAS 33.11] • 稀释每股收益的目标与基本每股收益一致——提供衡量每一普通股在主体业绩中所占权益的指标——同时考虑报告期内所有已发行的潜在稀释性普通股的影响。[IAS 33.32]

**中文:** 其基本逻辑在于，每股收益（包括稀释每股收益）应作为一项历史业绩衡量指标。

**中文:** 这尤其影响稀释每股收益的报告，使其偏离了另一目的：警示潜在的未来稀释。事实上，这些不同目标之间的张力在准则中显而易见。如下文第6.4.6节更详细讨论的那样，IAS 33为将某些潜在稀释性股份纳入稀释每股收益计算设定了非常严格的制度。然而，稀释每股收益仅需考虑那些会稀释持续经营收益的潜在股份（见下文第6.3.1节），这似乎更带有前瞻性的“警示信号”意味。终止经营在第5章中讨论。每股收益 2.2 范围 IAS 33适用于： (a) 主体的单独或个别财务报表： (i) 其普通股或潜在普通股在公开市场（国内或国外证券交易所或场外市场，包括地方和区域市场）交易；或 (ii) 为在公开市场发行普通股之目的，向证券委员会或其他监管机构提交或正在提交其财务报表的主体；以及 (b) 拥有母公司的集团的合并财务报表： (i) 其普通股或潜在普通股在公开市场（国内或国外证券交易所或场外市场，包括地方和区域市场）交易；或 (ii) 为在公开市场发行普通股之目的，向证券委员会或其他监管机构提交或正在提交其财务报表的母公司。[IAS 33.2] IAS 33也适用于任何披露每股收益的其他主体。[IAS 33.3] 当同时列报母公司的合并财务报表和单独财务报表时，该准则仅要求提供合并每股收益。

**中文:** 如果母公司选择基于其单独财务报表列报每股收益数据，该准则要求相关披露仅限于母公司单独的综合收益表（或单独利润表）表内，不得纳入合并财务报表。[IAS 33.4] 2014年1月，国际会计准则理事会发布了《IFRS 14——监管递延账户》。该准则允许范围内的首次采用者在采用国际财务报告准则时，在其首份IFRS财务报表中继续按照原公认会计原则核算监管递延账户余额。该准则对部分原公认会计原则中关于监管递延账户余额的会计实务引入了有限变更，主要涉及这些账户的列报方式。对于适用IFRS 14的主体，需要额外披露某些每股收益信息。相关内容在第6章第5.22.6.B节讨论。每股收益 基本每股收益 IAS 33要求计算归属于普通股股东的损益（如列报，则为持续经营损益）的基本每股收益。[IAS 33.9] 该准则对基本每股收益的定义（或更确切地说是描述）如下：[IAS 33.10] IAS 33摘录 基本每股收益应通过将归属于母公司普通股股东的损益（分子）除以当期发行在外普通股的加权平均数（分母）计算得出。3.1 收益 确定基本每股收益计算中使用的收益数字（包括总收益以及适用情况下的持续经营收益）的起点，是归属于母公司普通股股东的当期净利润或净亏损。[IAS 33.12]

**中文:** 根据《国际会计准则第1号——财务报表列报》（以及一旦《国际财务报告准则第18号》被应用，最迟自2027年1月1日或之后开始的期间起，依据该准则），这将包括所有收入和费用项目，包括归类为负债的优先股股息及税费，并在扣除非控制性权益后列报。[IAS 33.13, IAS 33.A1]。随后，需针对优先股股息的税后金额、优先股清偿产生的差额，以及归类为权益的优先股的其他类似影响进行调整。[IAS 33.12]。这些调整将在下文第5.2节中讨论。

**中文:** 3.2 股份数量 普通股被定义为“一种次于所有其他类别权益工具的权益工具”。[IAS 33.5]。权益工具的含义与《国际会计准则第32号——金融工具：列报》中的定义相同，即“证明在扣除所有负债后对主体资产享有剩余利益的任何合同”。[IAS 33.8, IAS 32.11]。《国际会计准则第33号》进一步指出，普通股仅在优先股等其他类型股份参与分配后，方可参与当期利润分配。[IAS 33.6]。该准则还明确，可能存在多类普通股，并要求对每一类在当期利润分配中享有不同权利的股份，分别计算和列报每股收益。[IAS 33.6, IAS 33.66]。实务中，通常可直接确定哪些工具属于每股收益计算中的普通股。不同类别股份的处理将在下文第5.4节中讨论。

**中文:** 《国际会计准则第33号》的基本规则是，所有发行在外的普通股均应纳入基本每股收益的计算——对于当期内的变动需进行时间加权（普通股变动将在下文第4节讨论）。[IAS 33.19]。

**中文:** 对此存在三项例外：• 已发行但尚未缴足股款的普通股，应根据其相对于全额缴付股份的股利参与权，按股份比例计入加权平均数（例如，若虽仅部分缴款但在股利分配上享有同等权利，则应全额计入）。[IAS 33.A15] • 库藏股在财务报表中作为权益的抵减项列示，在持有期间不计入每股收益计算的流通股数。尽管准则未明确表述，但该要求显然符合逻辑（因股份虽在法律上仍属已发行，但会计处理视同已赎回），并在准则附录的示例中予以说明（见下文第4.1节示例4-1）。[IAS 33.IE2] • 附或有返还条件（即可被召回）的股份，在召回条件解除前不视为流通股，因此在该期间内不计入基本每股收益。[IAS 33.24] 准则就新发行普通股何时应视为流通股提供了具体指引。

**中文:** 一般而言，股份应从对价可收取之日（准则通常视其为股份发行日）起纳入计算，例如：[IAS 33.21] • 为换取现金而发行的股份，应在现金可收取时纳入 • 因普通股或优先股股息自愿再投资而发行的股份，应在股息再投资时纳入 • 因债务工具转换为普通股而发行的股份，应自利息停止计提之日起纳入 • 为替代其他金融工具的利息或本金而发行的股份，应自利息停止计提之日起纳入

**中文:** 每股收益 • 为清偿主体负债而发行的股份，应自清偿日起纳入 • 作为收购非现金资产对价而发行的股份，应自收购确认之日起纳入 • 为换取向主体提供服务而发行的股份，应随服务提供进度纳入 • 因强制转换工具转换而将发行的股份，应自合同签订之日起纳入基本每股收益的计算 [IAS 33.23]

**中文:** 上述多数规定较为直接，但部分条款值得关注。为换取服务而发行的股份将依据IFRS 2进行会计处理，相关费用计入损益，同时贷记权益。IAS 33对如何将此类潜在股份纳入稀释每股收益提供了指引（参见下文第6.4.5节）；然而，对于“随服务提供进度纳入”的具体含义并未进一步阐述。该表述隐含之意似乎是，相关股份在服务提供过程中无条件归属。基于此，尽管股份实际发行时间可能不同，但显然应在股份权利归属时将其纳入基本每股收益计算。

**中文:** 然而，一种非常常见的股份支付薪酬形式涉及在长期期末基于未来事件（通常为持续雇佣关系，有时还包括特定未来业绩）满足条件后方可归属的股份。此类安排显然属于或有可发行股份，因此在归属前应排除在基本每股收益计算之外。事实上，在讨论稀释每股收益背景下的员工股份计划时，准则明确表述如下：[IAS 33.48]。摘自《国际会计准则第33号》：具有固定或可确定条款的员工股票期权及未归属普通股在计算稀释每股收益时视为期权处理，即使其可能取决于归属条件。这些工具在授予日即被视为已发行在外。基于业绩的员工股票期权则作为或有可发行股份处理，因其发行除时间推移外还需满足特定条件。或有可发行股份将在下文第6.4.6节讨论。关于最后一点，准则未定义强制性可转换工具的具体含义。一种观点认为，要求自初始确认起将股份计入每股收益，必然意味着该工具在初始确认时已收到对价。基于此，该规定应排除远期发行股份合同（根据上述第一点要求，此类合同仅在现金可收取时才能增加基本每股收益的分母）。同理，在远期回购普通股的反向情形中，股份仅应在对价应付时从基本每股收益中剔除。另一种观点则认为，所有具有约束力的发行或回购普通股协议，均应在主体成为协议方时反映于基本每股收益中。

**中文:** 另一个潜在的复杂问题是，在计算每股收益时，是否应对股份的发行与赎回采用对称处理。虽然这看似合乎逻辑，但在所有情况下并非毫无争议，尤其是考虑到《国际会计准则第32号》对某些自身权益衍生工具所要求的非对称会计处理（见第43章第5节讨论）。更广泛而言，该准则进一步指出，纳入时点由附带的条款和条件决定，且必须充分考虑与发行相关的任何合同实质。[《国际会计准则第33号》第21段] 仅在满足特定条件时方可发行的普通股（或有可发行股份），应自所有必要条件均已满足之日（即不再具有或然性时）起，才纳入基本每股收益的计算。[《国际会计准则第33号》第24段] 该规定被严格解释，如准则附录示例7所示（见下文第6.4.6.A节图示6-7）。在该示例中，某年度盈利若达到特定门槛，将触发股份发行。由于该条件直至年度最后一天（盈利确切可知时）才确定是否满足，因此新股在次年之前不得纳入基本每股收益。若股份将在未来某一日期（即仅随时间推移）发行，则《国际会计准则第33号》不将其视为或有可发行股份，因为时间推移具有确定性。[《国际会计准则第33号》第24段] 原则上，这似乎意味着此类股份应从协议日起纳入基本每股收益。然而，仍需根据具体事实和情况审慎判断。

**中文:** 基本每股收益的计算通常较为简单，但可能出现若干复杂情况，这些情况可从以下两个角度加以考量： （a） 已发行普通股数量的变动 （b） 影响分子的因素 以下两节将分别讨论上述内容。每股收益 已发行普通股变动 已发行普通股数量变动可能发生在多种情形下，下文将阐述最常见的情况。当会计期间内发生此类变动时，需对该期间每股收益计算中的股份数量进行调整；此外，在某些情况下，还需重新计算以前期间的每股收益。4.1 股份加权平均数 IAS 33隐含的方法论认为，主体资本（或更确切地说，其所反映的创收资产）与收益之间存在关联性。因此，为计算作为业绩衡量指标的每股收益，需调整分母中的股份数量，以反映期间内可用于产生该期间收益的资本变动。该准则指出，采用期间内已发行普通股的加权平均数，能够反映股东资本金额因不同时点已发行股份数量增减而可能发生的变动。期间内已发行普通股的加权平均数，是指期初已发行普通股数量，经期间内回购或发行的普通股数量乘以时间权重系数调整后的结果。时间权重系数是指股份发行在外天数占期间总天数的比例；IAS 33指出，在许多情况下，采用加权平均数的合理近似值即可满足要求。[IAS 33.20]

**中文:** 股份合并中重述所有期间发行在外股份数量的常规规则，在整体效果为按公允价值回购股份时不适用，因为在此类情况下，股份的减少源于资源的相应减少。此时，加权平均股份数需自特别股利确认之日起针对合并进行调整。[IAS 33.29] 4.3.3 配股 配股是主体通过资本市场获取额外资本的常用方法。根据此类发行的条款，现有股东有权按其现有持股比例认购主体新增股份。配股股份通常按当前市价或低于市价的价格发行。若按市价发行，其对每股收益的处理方式与上述第4.1节所述相同。然而，当配股价低于市价时，情况则较为复杂，因为此类发行相当于红股发行（见上述第4.3.1节）与按市价全额发行的结合。在此类情况下，国际会计准则第33号要求对配股前发行在外的股份数量进行调整，以反映其中包含的红利因素。[IAS 33.26, IAS 33.27] 适用于所有现有股东的配股红利因素由以下调整因子（有时称为红利分数）确定：[IAS 33.A2] 行使配股权前每股公允价值 理论除权后每股公允价值 行使配股权前每股公允价值是指包含配股权认购未来股份权利的股份实际报价。若配股权与股份分开交易，则使用的公允价值为股份含权交易的最后一个交易日的收盘价。

**中文:** 每股收益 稀释每股收益 6.1 稀释每股收益的必要性 列报基本每股收益旨在通过计算主体在当期已发行每股股份所赚取的利润，展示一项业绩指标。主体通常会在未来承诺发行股份，这将导致基本每股收益发生变化。《国际会计准则第33号》将此类承诺称为潜在普通股，并将其定义为"可能使其持有者有权获得普通股的金融工具或其他合同"[《国际会计准则第33号》第5段]。《国际会计准则第33号》给出的潜在普通股示例包括： (a) 可转换为普通股的金融负债或权益工具（包括优先股）； (b) 期权和认股权证（无论根据《国际会计准则第32号》还是《国际财务报告准则第2号》进行会计处理）； (c) 因满足合同安排（如购买企业或其他资产）所产生条件而将发行的股份[《国际会计准则第33号》第7段]。 当潜在股份实际发行时，对基本每股收益的影响将体现在两方面：首先，已发行股份数量将发生变化；其次，利润可能受到影响，例如因利息费用降低或现金流入产生的回报。

**中文:** 对基本每股收益产生不利调整的情形，准则将其描述为稀释，定义为"假设可转换工具被转换、期权或认股权证被行使、或在满足特定条件时发行普通股，所导致的每股收益减少或每股亏损增加"。[IAS 33.5]。这种每股收益的潜在下降通过计算稀释每股收益来量化，因此： (a) 归属于权益持有人的损益，应加上当期就稀释性潜在普通股确认的股利和利息的税后金额，并针对因转换稀释性潜在普通股而导致的任何其他收入或费用变动进行调整。以及 (b) 发行在外普通股的加权平均数，应加上假设所有稀释性潜在普通股被转换时本应发行在外的额外普通股的加权平均数。[IAS 33.32]。

**中文:** 6.2 稀释每股收益的计算 IAS 33要求计算归属于母公司普通股权益持有人的损益的稀释每股收益，若列报，还需计算归属于他们的持续经营损益。[IAS 33.30]。为此，归属于普通股权益持有人的损益及发行在外普通股的加权平均数，需针对所有潜在普通股的影响进行调整。[IAS 33.31]。在计算稀释每股收益时，所使用的股数为计算基本每股收益时使用的股数，加上所有稀释性潜在普通股转换为普通股时本应发行的股份的加权平均数。与基本每股收益计算中发行在外股份的处理相同，潜在普通股应按其发行在外的期间进行加权。[IAS 33.36, IAS 33.38]。

**中文:** 因此，潜在普通股： • 被视为在期初已转换为普通股，若期初不存在，则在其发行日转换 [IAS 33.36] • 被注销或允许失效的潜在普通股，仅在其存续期间计入 • 在期间内转换为普通股的潜在普通股，计入至转换日（自转换日起将计入基本每股收益）[IAS 33.38]

**中文:** 稀释性潜在普通股的数量应针对每个列报期间独立确定，且后续不予重新调整。特别是，前期每股收益不因潜在股份转换为普通股的假设变化而重述。IAS 33 同时强调，年初至今期间包含的稀释性潜在普通股数量，并非各中期计算中包含的稀释性潜在普通股的加权平均数。[IAS 33.37, IAS 33.65] 这一点在 IAS 33 所附的综合示例中予以说明，其核心内容已转载至本章附录。

**中文:** 每股收益 6.2.1 稀释每股收益 收益数字采用基本每股收益的收益数据，并针对期间内潜在流通股份实际发行时可能产生的任何变化进行调整。

**中文:** 调整应针对以下各项的税后影响进行： (a) 在计算基本每股收益所用利润数字时扣除的、与稀释性潜在普通股相关的任何股利或其他项目 (b) 当期确认的、与稀释性潜在普通股相关的任何利息 (c) 因转换稀释性潜在普通股而导致的任何其他收入或费用变动 [IAS 33.33] 这些调整还将包括根据《国际财务报告准则第9号——金融工具》规定的实际利率法，因在工具存续期内分摊交易成本、溢价或折价而收取的任何金额。[IAS 33.34] 具有结算方式选择权的工具也可能需要对分子进行调整，详见下文第6.2.2节。该准则指出，直接归属于工具的某些收益调整也可能影响其他收入或费用项目，需予以考虑。例如，可转换债务转换后利息费用降低，可能导致利润分享计划下的费用增加。[IAS 33.35] 对于行使股票期权或认股权证时将收到的收益，不考虑任何推算收益。此类潜在普通股对稀释每股收益的影响反映在分母的计算中，详见下文第6.4.2节。

**中文:** 6.2.2 稀释后的股份数量 《国际会计准则第33号》讨论了若干特定类型的潜在普通股及其纳入计算的方式；这些内容将在下文第6.4节中阐述。更广泛而言，该准则还讨论了潜在普通股转换或结算方式由一方酌情决定的情形，具体如下： (a) 转换时将发行的股份数量必须根据潜在普通股的条款确定。

**中文:** 当存在多种转换基础时，计算应采用潜在普通股持有方角度下最有利的转换率或行权价格。[IAS 33.39] (b) 当主体发行了可选择以股份或现金结算的合同时，应假定该合同将以股份结算。若由此产生的潜在普通股具有稀释效应，则应将其计入稀释每股收益。[IAS 33.58] 若此类合同在会计处理中被确认为资产或负债，或同时包含权益成分和负债成分，则需调整分子，以反映假设该合同被完全归类为权益工具时期间内本应产生的损益变动。该调整与上文第6.2.1节所述的调整类似。[IAS 33.59] (c) 对于持有方可选择以普通股或现金结算的合同，在计算稀释每股收益时，应采用现金结算与股份结算中稀释效应更强的方式。[IAS 33.60] 上述(b)项所涵盖工具的一个示例为：到期时赋予发行方无限制选择权以现金或其自身普通股结算本金的债务工具（参见下文第6.4.1.A节中的示例6-3）。上述(c)项所涵盖工具的一个示例为：赋予持有方选择以普通股或现金结算权利的签出看跌期权。[IAS 33.61] 我们认为，上述(b)项和(c)项中关于结算选择权的准则要求存在一定混淆。特别是，这些要求似乎设想了一种基于严格法律形式（现金或股份）的二元会计模型。然而，IAS 32针对三种不同结算方式——净现金结算、净股份结算及实物总额结算（详见第43章第5节）——制定了相应规则。

**中文:** 上述情况的一个后果是，若严格按字面理解，只有当存在结算方式选择权时，才需调整分子以剔除因负债产生的收入或费用项目。然而，根据《国际会计准则第32号》，强制性净额股份结算同样会产生负债及收入/费用。我们认为，在计算稀释每股收益时，任何此类利润表项目均需剔除。 每股收益 我们的观点 尽管《国际会计准则第33号》未明确说明，但我们认为，稀释每股收益的分子必须针对与强制性净额股份结算相关的潜在普通股所产生的任何损益影响进行调整。 6.3 稀释性潜在普通股 仅当潜在股份的发行会对每股收益产生稀释效应时，才将其纳入计算。若潜在普通股转换为普通股会导致每股收益增加或每股亏损减少，则此类股份具有"反稀释性"。[《国际会计准则第33号》第5段、第43段] 计算稀释每股收益时，不得假设转换、行权或发行会对每股收益产生反稀释效应的潜在普通股。[《国际会计准则第33号》第43段] 该准则提供了详细指引，用于确定哪些潜在股份被视为具有稀释性，从而纳入稀释每股收益的计算。该指引涵盖需被稀释以触发纳入的利润要素，以及测试潜在股份以确定累计稀释效应的顺序。以下分别讨论。 6.3.1 以持续经营损益判断稀释性 潜在普通股仅在其转换为普通股会导致持续经营每股收益减少或每股亏损增加时，才应被视为具有稀释性。

**中文:** 此处的“控制数”关注的是持续经营业务的净结果，即归属于母公司股东的净利润或净亏损，需扣除与优先股相关的项目（参见上文第5.2节），并排除与终止经营相关的项目。[IAS 33.42]。计算持续经营业务的稀释每股收益和总稀释每股收益时，必须使用相同的分母。通过参考潜在股份对持续经营每股收益的影响来确定应纳入哪些潜在股份，可能会对总每股收益产生一些略微奇特的结果。例如，有可能排除会稀释基本每股收益（但不会稀释持续经营每股收益）的工具，同时纳入对总利润具有反稀释作用的项目。准则对后一点作了如下说明。[IAS 33.A3]。摘自《国际会计准则第33号》控制数 A3 为说明第42段和第43段所述控制数概念的应用，假设某实体归属于母公司股东的持续经营业务利润为CU4,800，归属于母公司股东的终止经营业务亏损为(CU7,200)，归属于母公司股东的亏损为(CU2,400)，且发行在外普通股为2,000股，潜在普通股为400股。该实体的基本每股收益为：持续经营业务CU2.40，终止经营业务(CU3.60)，亏损(CU1.20)。这400股潜在普通股被纳入稀释每股收益计算，因为假设这400股潜在普通股对利润或亏损无影响，则由此产生的持续经营业务每股收益CU2.00具有稀释性。

**中文:** 由于归属于母公司股东的持续经营利润是控制性数字，因此该实体在计算其他每股收益金额时也包含了那400股潜在普通股，即使由此产生的每股收益金额相对于其可比的基本每股收益金额具有反稀释作用，即每股亏损更小[持续经营亏损为每股(3.00)货币单位，总亏损为每股(1.00)货币单位]。 6.3.2 以潜在股份的累积影响判断稀释性 当实体拥有多种不同的潜在普通股时，在判断其是否具有稀释性（从而反映在计算中）时，应按照从最具稀释性到最不具稀释性的顺序依次考虑每一批或每一系列潜在普通股。仅当这些潜在股份产生累积稀释效应时，才应将其纳入计算。这意味着，某些单独考虑时会稀释基本每股收益的潜在股份可能需要被排除在外。这导致稀释每股收益反映了基本每股收益的最大总体稀释程度。该准则指出，期权和认股权证通常首先被纳入，因为它们不影响稀释每股收益计算中的分子（但请参见下文第6.4.2节的讨论）。[IAS 33.44] 具体操作方法如下例所示。[IAS 33.IE9]

**中文:** 这将允许计算累计业绩门槛，并与截至目前的实际收益进行比较。如果在第二季度末，预计当年年通胀率为5%，则目标将变为2,100,000货币单位，因此第二季度稀释每股收益中将包含200,000股。鉴于准则缺乏明确性，实践中似乎可能选择上述任一方法。每股收益 6.4.6.B 基于股价的或有事项 此处的规定更为直接。在这些情况下，如果影响是稀释性的，稀释每股收益的计算基于假设报告期末的市价即为或有事项期末的市价时所发行的股份数量。如果条件基于报告期末之后一段时间内的平均市价，则使用已过去期间的平均值。同样，准则解释称，由于未来期间市价可能发生变化，基本每股收益的计算在或有事项期末之前不包含此类或有可发行普通股，因为并非所有必要条件均已满足。[IAS 33.54]。 6.4.6.C 其他或有事项 关于非由收益或股价驱动的或有事项的要求如下：“假设条件的当前状态在或有事项期末之前保持不变，则根据报告期末的状态，将或有可发行普通股纳入稀释每股收益的计算。”[IAS 33.56]。准则通过一个示例说明“其他或有事项”规则，该示例涉及根据特定数量零售门店的开业情况发行股份，此类或有事项包含在准则的数值示例中（见上文第6.4.6.A节中的示例6-7）。

**中文:** 与上述基于盈利的或有事项类似，此类条件通常被视为以累计门槛形式表述，该门槛可能在报告期末达成或未达成。因此，若条件表述为达到特定年均店铺开业数量，所需处理方式相同。 6.4.7 被投资单位的潜在普通股 子公司、合营企业或联营企业可能向母公司或对被投资单位具有共同控制或重大影响的投资者以外的其他方发行潜在普通股，这些股份可转换为子公司、合营企业或联营企业的普通股，或母公司或对被投资单位具有共同控制或重大影响的投资者（报告主体）的普通股。若这些子公司、合营企业或联营企业的潜在普通股对报告主体的基本每股收益具有稀释作用，则应将其纳入稀释每股收益的计算。[IAS 33.40] 该准则要求此类潜在普通股按以下方式纳入稀释每股收益的计算： (a) 子公司、合营企业或联营企业发行的、使其持有者能够获得该子公司、合营企业或联营企业普通股的工具，应纳入该子公司、合营企业或联营企业稀释每股收益数据的计算。随后，基于报告主体持有该子公司、合营企业或联营企业工具的情况，将这些每股收益数据纳入报告主体的每股收益计算。 (b) 子公司、合营企业或联营企业发行的、可转换为报告主体普通股的工具，在计算稀释每股收益时，应视为报告主体的潜在普通股。
