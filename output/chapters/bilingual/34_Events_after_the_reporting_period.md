Events after the reporting period are those events, both favourable and non-favourable, which occur between the last day of the reporting period and the date on which the financial statements are authorised for issue. IAS 10 divides such events into two categories: adjusting and non-adjusting events. Adjusting events provide evidence of conditions which existed at the end of the reporting period and, consequently, an entity is required to adjust the amounts recognised in its financial statements to reflect those events. This may require the recognition of a previously unrecognised item. Conversely, no adjustment is made for non-adjusting events, though disclosure is required for such events, to the extent that the events are material to the financial statements. [IAS 10.21]. IAS 10 does not permit an entity to prepare its financial statements on a going concern basis if events after the reporting period indicate that the going concern assumption is no longer appropriate. What you need to know • IAS 10 identifies two types of events after the reporting period: adjusting and non-adjusting. • Adjusting events are events which provide evidence of conditions which existed at the end of the reporting period; amounts recognised in financial statements are adjusted for such events. • Non-adjusting events are indicative of conditions which arose after the reporting period, but before the financial statements were authorised for issue; amounts recognised in the financial statements are not adjusted for such events, but those events are required to be disclosed, to the extent material. • When the going concern basis of accounting becomes inappropriate after the end of the reporting period, an entity is required to change its basis of accounting. [IAS 10.2, IAS 10.3, IAS 10.7]. This definition includes all events occurring between those dates [IAS 10.7]. The principal issue is determining which of these events are required to be reflected in the financial statements as adjustments, which are material enough to require additional disclosure, and which require neither adjustment nor disclosure. The following timeline illustrates events after the end of the reporting period that are within the scope of IAS 10 for an entity with a 31 December year-end: The financial statements of an entity present, among other things, its financial position at the end of the reporting period. Therefore, it is appropriate to adjust the financial statements for all events that offer greater clarity about conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period (reporting date), and which occur prior to the date the financial statements are authorised for issue. The standard, therefore, requires entities to adjust the amounts recognised in the financial statements for ‘adjusting events’, events that provide evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period. [IAS 10.3(a), IAS 10.8]. By the same reasoning an entity does not recognise in the financial statements those events that relate to conditions that arose after the reporting period (‘non-adjusting events’). However, if non-adjusting events are material (that is, non-disclosure of the event could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that the primary users of general purpose financial statements make on the basis of those financial statements), certain disclosures are required. [IAS 10.3(b), IAS 10.10, IAS 10.21, IAS 1.7, IFRS 18 Appendix A, IAS 8.5, IAS 8(2027).5]. One exception to the general rule for non-adjusting events is when the going concern basis becomes inappropriate. This is always treated as an adjusting event. [IAS 10.1, IAS 10.14, IAS 10.15]. The requirements of IAS 10 and some practical issues resulting from these requirements are dealt with in section 2 and section 3 below, respectively. Differences between the IFRS accounting standards and US GAAP guidance on this topic are not addressed in this chapter as the standards are not converged. Therefore, differences between IFRS accounting standards and US GAAP may exist that are not discussed in this chapter. The standard observes that the process for authorising financial statements for issue varies depending upon the management structure, statutory requirements and procedures followed in preparing and finalising the financial statements. [IAS 10.4]. The standard sets out two particular examples of different meanings of ‘authorised for issue’ as follows: (a) An entity may be required to submit its financial statements to its shareholders for approval after the financial statements have been issued. (b) The management of an entity may be required to issue its financial statements to a supervisory board (made up solely of non-executives) for approval. Events after the reporting period If an entity re-issues financial statements (whether to correct an error or to include events that occurred after the financial statements were originally authorised for issue), there is a new date of authorisation for issue. The financial statements must then reflect, up to the new date of authorisation for issue: • All adjusting events, by updating the amounts recognised in the financial statements • Non-adjusting events, through additional disclosure However, in certain circumstances, the re-issuing of previously issued financial statements is required by local regulators, particularly for inclusion in public offering and similar documents. In November 2012, the IFRS IC was asked to clarify the accounting implications of applying IAS 10 when previously issued financial statements are re-issued in connection with an offering document.2 In May 2013, the IFRS IC responded that: • The scope of IAS 10 is the accounting for, and disclosure of, events after the reporting period and that the objective of this Standard is to prescribe both of the following: (a) When an entity should adjust its financial statements for events after the reporting period (b) The disclosures that an entity should give about the date when the financial statements were authorised for issue and about events after the reporting period • Financial statements prepared in accordance with IAS 10 should reflect all adjusting and non-adjusting events up to the date that the financial statements were authorised for issue. • IAS 10 does not address the presentation of re-issued financial statements in an offering document when the originally issued financial statements have not been withdrawn, but the re-issued financial statements are provided either as supplementary information or a re-presentation of the original financial statements in an offering document in accordance with regulatory requirements. The IFRS IC decided not to add this issue to its agenda on the basis of the response above and because the issue arises in multiple jurisdictions, each with particular securities laws and regulations which may dictate the form for re-presentations of financial statements.3 Accordingly, depending on the particular regulatory requirements in a given jurisdiction, there may be variations in how re-issued financial statements are presented. 2.1.2 Adjusting events Adjusting events are “those that provide evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period.” [IAS 10.3(a)]. Examples of adjusting events are as follows: (a) The settlement after the reporting period of a court case that confirms that the entity had a present obligation at the end of the reporting period. In this situation, an entity adjusts any previously recognised provision related to this court case in accordance with IAS 37 Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets or recognises a new provision. Mere disclosure of a contingent liability is not sufficient because the settlement provides additional evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period that would give rise to a provision in accordance with IAS 37 (see # Chapter 27 at sections 3.1.1 and 3.2.1). (b) The receipt of information after the reporting period indicating that an asset was impaired at the end of the reporting period, or that the amount of a previously recognised impairment loss for that asset needs to be adjusted. For example: (i) The bankruptcy of a customer that occurs after the reporting period usually confirms that the customer was credit-impaired at the end of the reporting period (this is discussed further in section 3.3 below). (ii) The sale of inventories after the reporting period may give evidence about their net realisable value at the end of the reporting period. (c) The determination after the reporting period of the cost of assets purchased, or the proceeds from assets sold, before the end of the reporting period. (d) The determination after the reporting period of the amount of profit-sharing or bonus payments, if the entity had a present legal or constructive obligation at the end of the reporting period to make such payments as a result of events before that date (see Chapter 31 at section 12.3). (e) The discovery of fraud or errors that show that the financial statements are incorrect (see section 3.4 below). [IAS 10.9]. Events after the reporting period 2.1.3.A Dividend declaration Extract from IAS 10 Dividends If an entity declares dividends to holders of equity instruments (as defined in IAS 32 Financial Instruments: Presentation) after the reporting period, the entity shall not recognise those dividends as a liability at the end of the reporting period. If dividends are declared after the reporting period but before the financial statements are authorised for issue, the dividends are not recognised as a liability at the end of the reporting period because no obligation exists at that time. Such dividends are disclosed in the notes in accordance with IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements. In respect of dividend declarations, as in (l) in section 2.1.3 above, dividends are only recognised as a liability if declared by the end of the reporting period. [IAS 10.12, IAS 10.13, IAS 10(2027).13]. As a consequential amendment to IAS 10, the definition of ‘declared’ in this context was moved to IFRIC 17 Distributions of Non-cash Assets to Owners. IFRIC 17 did not change the principle regarding the appropriate timing for the recognition of dividends payable. [IFRIC 17.BC18, IFRIC 17.BC19, IFRIC 17.BC20]. It states that an entity recognises a liability to pay a dividend when the dividend is appropriately authorised and is no longer at the discretion of the entity, which is the date: • When declaration of the dividend, e.g., by management or the board of directors, is approved by the relevant authority, e.g., the shareholders, if the jurisdiction requires such approval Or • When the dividend is declared, e.g., by management or the board of directors, if the jurisdiction does not require further approval [IFRIC 17.10]. How we see it In many jurisdictions, the directors may keep discretion to cancel an interim dividend until such time as it is paid. In this case, the interim dividend is not declared (within the meaning described above), and is, therefore, not recognised until paid. Final dividends proposed by directors, in many jurisdictions, are only binding when approved by shareholders in general meeting or by the members passing a written resolution. Therefore, such a final dividend is only recognised as a liability when declared, i.e., approved by the shareholders at the annual general meeting or through the passing of a resolution by the members of an entity. IAS 10 contains a reminder that an entity discloses dividends, both proposed and declared after the reporting period but before the financial statements are authorised for issue, in the notes to the financial statements in accordance with IAS 1 (see Chapter 3 at section 5.6). [IAS 1.137, IFRS 18.132, IAS 10.13, IAS 10(2027).13]. Similar issues arise regarding the declaration of dividends by subsidiaries, associates and other equity investments. Although IAS 10 does not specifically address such items, IFRS 9 Financial Instruments requires a shareholder to recognise dividends when the shareholder’s right to receive payment is established, it is probable that the economic benefits associated with the dividend will flow to the shareholder and the dividend can be measured reliably (see Chapter 46 at section 2.5). [IFRS 9.5.7.1A]. Similarly, IAS 27 Separate Financial Statements also contains this general principle in recognising in an entity’s separate financial statements those dividends received from subsidiaries, joint ventures or associates when its right to receive the dividend is established (see Chapter 9 at section 2.4.1). [IAS 27.12]. Accordingly, a shareholder does not recognise such dividend income until the period in which the dividend is declared. Events after the reporting period 2.2 The treatment of adjusting events 2.2.1 Events requiring adjustment to the amounts recognised, or disclosures, in the financial statements IAS 10 requires that the amounts recognised in the financial statements be adjusted to take account of an adjusting event. [IAS 10.8]. The standard also notes that an entity may receive information after the reporting period about conditions existing at the end of the reporting period relating to disclosures made in the financial statements but not affecting the amounts recognised in them. [IAS 10.20]. In such cases, the standard requires the entity to update the disclosures that relate to those conditions for the new information. [IAS 10.19]. For example, evidence may become available after the reporting period about a contingent liability that existed at the end of the reporting period. In addition to considering whether to recognise or change a provision under IAS 37, IAS 10 requires an entity to update its disclosures about the contingent liability for that evidence. [IAS 10.20]. 2.2.2 Events indicating that the going concern basis is not appropriate If management determines after the reporting period (but before the financial statements are authorised for issue) either that it intends to liquidate the entity or to cease trading, or that it has no realistic alternative but to do so, the financial statements must not be prepared on a going concern basis. [IAS 10.14]. Deterioration in operating results and financial position after the reporting period may indicate a need to consider whether the going concern assumption is still appropriate. If the going concern assumption is no longer appropriate, the standard states that the effect is so pervasive that it results in a fundamental change in the basis of accounting, rather than an adjustment to the amounts recognised within the original basis of accounting. [IAS 10.15]. As discussed in Chapter 3 at section 4.1.2.B, IFRS accounting standards contains no guidance on this ‘fundamental change in the basis of accounting’. Accordingly, entities will need to consider carefully their individual circumstances to arrive at an appropriate basis. The standard also contains a reminder of the specific disclosure requirements under IAS 1: (a) When the financial statements are not prepared on a going concern basis, that fact must be disclosed, together with the basis on which the financial statements have been prepared and the reason why the entity is not regarded as a going concern. (b) When management is aware of material uncertainties related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt upon the entity’s ability to continue as a going concern, disclosure of those uncertainties must be made. [IAS 10.16(a), IAS 10(2027).16(a), IAS 10.16(b), IAS 10(2027).16(b), IAS 1.25, **IAS 8(2027).6K, IAS 1.26, IAS 8(2027).6L].** While IFRS accounting standards are generally written from the perspective that an entity is a going concern, they are also applicable when another basis of accounting is used to prepare financial statements. Various IFRS accounting standards acknowledge that financial statements may be prepared on either a going concern basis or an alternative basis of accounting. [IAS 1.25, IAS 8(2027).6K, IAS 10.14, CF 3.9]. Such IFRS accounting standards do not specifically exclude the application of IFRS accounting standards when an alternative basis of accounting is used. As a result, financial statements prepared on a ‘non-going concern’ basis of accounting may be described as complying with IFRS accounting standards as long as that other basis of preparation is sufficiently described. Events after the reporting period Practical issues The standard does address certain practical matters through examples of adjusting and non-adjusting events. For example, the standard asserts that the bankruptcy of a customer that occurs after the reporting period would usually be an adjusting event (see section 2.1.2 above). It also states that a decline in fair value of investments after the reporting period does not normally relate to conditions at the end of the reporting period and therefore, would be a non-adjusting event (see section 2.1.3 above). Judgement of the facts and circumstances is required to determine whether an event that occurs after the reporting period provides evidence about a condition that existed at the end of the reporting period, or whether the condition arose subsequent to the reporting period. The following sections address some commonly encountered practical issues. 3.1 Valuation of inventory The sale of inventories after the reporting period is normally a good indicator of their net realisable value (NRV) at that date. IAS 10 states that such sales “may give evidence about their net realisable value at the end of the reporting period”. [IAS 10.9(b)(ii)]. However, in some cases, NRV decreases because of conditions that did not exist at the end of the reporting period. Therefore, it is important to determine why NRV decreased. Did it decrease because of circumstances that existed at the end of the reporting period, which subsequently became known, or did it decrease because of circumstances that arose after the end of the reporting period? A decrease in price is merely a response to changing conditions so it is important to assess the reasons for, and timing of, these changes. Some examples of changing conditions are as follows: (a) Price reductions caused by a sudden increase in cheap imports Whilst it is arguable that the ‘dumping’ of cheap imports after the reporting period is a condition that arises subsequent to that date, it is more likely that this is a reaction to a condition that already existed such as overproduction in other parts of the world. Thus, it might be more appropriate in such a situation to adjust the value of inventories based on its subsequent NRV. (b) Price reductions caused by increased competition The reasons for price reductions and increased competition do not generally arise overnight but normally occur over a period. For example, a competitor may have built up a competitive advantage by investing in machinery that is more efficient. In these circumstances, it is appropriate for an entity to adjust the valuation of its inventories because this situation existed at the end of the reporting period. (c) Price reductions caused by the introduction of an improved competitive product It is unlikely that a competitor developed and introduced an improved product overnight. Therefore, it is appropriate to adjust the valuation of inventories held at the end of the reporting period to their NRV after that introduction because the entity’s failure to maintain its competitive position in relation to product improvements existed at the end of the reporting period. Price reductions after the reporting period caused by increased competition are generally evidence of conditions that existed at the end of the reporting period. For certain types of inventory, however, there may be clear evidence of a price at the end of the reporting period. If this is the case, it is inappropriate to adjust the NRV of that inventory to reflect a subsequent decline. An example is inventories for which there is a price on an active commodities market. In addition, inventory may be physically damaged or destroyed after the reporting period (e.g., by fire, flood, or other disaster). In these cases, the entity does not adjust the financial statements. However, the entity may be required to disclose the subsequent decline in NRV of the inventories if the impact is material (see section 2.3 above). 3.2 Over time measurement of progress Events after the reporting period frequently give evidence about the profitability of contracts with customers, which are in progress at the end of the reporting period and where revenue is recognised over time. IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers requires an assessment to be made of the progress towards complete satisfaction of performance obligations satisfied over time (see Chapter 28 at section 7.1.4). [IFRS 15.40]. ## ## 报告期后事项概述 《国际会计准则第10号——报告期后事项》¹规定了财务报表必须就报告期后发生的事项进行调整的情形、主体必须披露的财务报表批准报出日，以及与报告期后事项相关的必要披露。报告期后事项是指发生在报告期最后一日与财务报表批准报出日之间的所有有利和不利事项。《国际会计准则第10号》将此类事项分为两类：调整事项和非调整事项。

**中文:** 调整事项提供了报告期末已存在情况的证据，因此，主体需要调整其财务报表中已确认的金额以反映这些事项。这可能需要确认先前未确认的项目。相反，对于非调整事项不作调整，但若此类事项对财务报表具有重大影响，则需予以披露。[IAS 10.21] 如果报告期后事项表明持续经营假设不再适用，IAS 10不允许主体基于持续经营基础编制财务报表。需要了解的内容 • IAS 10明确了报告期后事项的两种类型：调整事项与非调整事项。 • 调整事项是提供报告期末已存在情况证据的事项；财务报表中已确认的金额需针对此类事项进行调整。 • 非调整事项表明报告期后、财务报表批准报出前出现的情况；财务报表中已确认的金额无需针对此类事项进行调整，但若具有重大影响，则需披露这些事项。 • 当报告期结束后持续经营会计基础不再适当时，主体需变更其会计基础。2024年4月，国际会计准则理事会（IASB）发布了《IFRS 18——财务报表的列报和披露》，该准则将取代《IAS 1——财务报表列报》，适用于自2027年1月1日或之后开始的报告期（允许提前采用），其中包含对IAS 10第13段和第16段的修订，仅涉及将IAS 1的引用替换为相应的更新版IFRS 18和IAS 8（2027年）《财务报表编制基础》的引用。

**中文:** IFRS 18《财务报表列报与披露》取代了IAS 1，适用于自2027年1月1日或之后开始的报告期间（允许提前采用），该准则由国际会计准则理事会（IASB）于2024年4月发布。有关IFRS 18新概念和要求的详细信息，请参见第4章。报告期后事项 2.1.3.A 股利宣告 摘自IAS 10《报告期后事项》 股利 如果实体在报告期后向权益工具（定义见IAS 32《金融工具：列报》）持有者宣告发放股利，则该实体不应在报告期末将这些股利确认为负债。如果股利在报告期后但在财务报表批准报出前宣告，则报告期末不将其确认为负债，因为此时不存在义务。此类股利应按照IAS 1《财务报表列报》在附注中披露。关于股利宣告，如上述第2.1.3节第(l)项所述，股利仅在报告期末之前宣告时才确认为负债。[IAS 10.12, IAS 10.13, IAS 10(2027).13]。作为对IAS 10的相应修订，此语境下“宣告”的定义已移至IFRIC 17《向所有者分配非现金资产》。IFRIC 17未改变关于应付股利确认时点的原则。[IFRIC 17.BC18, IFRIC 17.BC19, IFRIC 17.BC20]。

**中文:** 该准则规定，当股利已适当授权且企业不再拥有自主决定权时，企业应确认支付股利的负债，具体时点为： • 若司法管辖区要求相关机构（如股东）批准，则于股利宣告（例如由管理层或董事会宣告）经相关机构批准之日；或 • 若司法管辖区无需进一步批准，则于股利宣告（例如由管理层或董事会宣告）之日 [IFRIC 17.10]。

**中文:** **我们的理解** 在许多司法管辖区，董事可在中期股利实际支付前保留撤销该股利的自主决定权。在此情况下，中期股利并未被宣告（按上述定义），因此在实际支付前不予确认。 在许多司法管辖区，董事提议的期末股利仅在股东大会上经股东批准或由成员通过书面决议后方具有约束力。因此，此类期末股利仅于宣告时（即股东在年度股东大会上批准或企业成员通过决议时）确认为负债。 《国际会计准则第10号》提示，企业应根据《国际会计准则第1号》在财务报表附注中披露报告期后但财务报表批准报出前已提议或宣告的股利（见第3章第5.6节）[IAS 1.137, IFRS 18.132, IAS 10.13, IAS 10(2027).13]。 子公司、联营企业及其他权益性投资的股利宣告亦存在类似问题。尽管《国际会计准则第10号》未专门涉及此类事项，但《国际财务报告准则第9号——金融工具》要求股东在满足以下条件时确认股利：股东收取款项的权利已确立、与股利相关的经济利益很可能流入股东、且股利能够可靠计量（见第46章第2.5节）。

**中文:** [IFRS 9.5.7.1A] 类似地，《国际会计准则第27号——单独财务报表》也包含这一基本原则，即在主体单独财务报表中确认其从子公司、合营企业或联营企业收取的股利，前提是主体收取股利的权利已确立（参见第9章第2.4.1节）。[IAS 27.12] 因此，股东在股利宣告期间之前不得确认此类股利收入。

**中文:** **报告期后事项** **实务问题** 该准则通过调整事项与非调整事项的示例，确实涉及某些实务问题的处理。例如，准则指出，报告期后发生的客户破产通常属于调整事项（见上文第2.1.2节）。同时，准则规定，报告期后投资公允价值的下降通常与报告期末的状况无关，因此属于非调整事项（见上文第2.1.3节）。判断事实与情况时，需确定报告期后发生的事项是否提供了报告期末已存在状况的证据，抑或该状况是在报告期后产生的。以下各节将讨论一些常见的实务问题。

**中文:** **3.1 存货估值** 报告期后的存货销售通常是确定其可变现净值（NRV）的良好指标。《国际会计准则第10号》指出，此类销售"可能提供关于报告期末存货可变现净值的证据"。[IAS 10.9(b)(ii)]。然而，在某些情况下，可变现净值的下降是由于报告期末并不存在的状况所致。因此，确定可变现净值下降的原因至关重要：其下降是由于报告期末已存在但随后才被知晓的状况，还是由于报告期后新产生的状况？

**中文:** 价格下降仅仅是对条件变化的反应，因此评估这些变化的原因和时机至关重要。条件变化的示例如下： (a) 因廉价进口商品突然增加导致的价格下降 尽管有观点认为，报告期后“倾销”廉价进口商品属于该日期之后出现的状况，但更可能的是，这是对已存在状况（如世界其他地区的生产过剩）的反应。因此，在此类情况下，基于后续可变现净值调整存货价值可能更为恰当。 (b) 因竞争加剧导致的价格下降 价格下降和竞争加剧的原因通常不会在一夜之间出现，而是往往经历一段时间。例如，竞争对手可能通过投资更高效的机器建立了竞争优势。在这种情况下，企业调整存货估值是恰当的，因为该状况在报告期末已经存在。 (c) 因引入更具竞争力的改良产品导致的价格下降 竞争对手不太可能在一夜之间开发并推出改良产品。因此，将报告期末持有的存货估值调整至该产品推出后的可变现净值是恰当的，因为企业在产品改进方面未能维持竞争地位的状况在报告期末已经存在。 报告期后因竞争加剧导致的价格下降，通常可证明报告期末已存在的状况。然而，对于某些类型的存货，报告期末可能存在明确的价格证据。若如此，则不应调整该存货的可变现净值以反映后续下跌。

**中文:** 一个例子是存在活跃商品市场报价的存货。此外，存货可能在报告期后发生物理损坏或毁损（例如，因火灾、洪水或其他灾害）。在这些情况下，主体不应调整财务报表。但是，如果影响重大，主体可能需要披露存货可变现净值的后续下降（参见上文第2.3节）。 3.2 随时间推移计量履约进度 报告期后事项通常为报告期末正在履行且收入随时间确认的客户合同盈利能力提供证据。《国际财务报告准则第15号——客户合同收入》要求评估随时间履行的履约义务的完成进度（参见第28章第7.1.4节）。[IFRS 15.40] 在此评估中，必须考虑报告期后发生的事项，并确定其属于调整事项还是非调整事项，其财务影响应纳入随时间推移计量进度的方法中。 报告期后事项 3.3 债务人破产与《国际财务报告准则第9号》预期信用损失 债务人破产或无力偿债通常是一个逐步累积的过程。《国际会计准则第10号》指出，客户在报告期后破产通常证实该客户在报告期末已发生信用减值（《国际财务报告准则第9号》一般方法下的第三阶段——参见第47章第3.1节）。[IAS 10.9(b)(i)] 然而，若有证据表明债务人破产完全由报告期后发生的事项所致，则该事项为非调整事项。若影响重大，主体需披露债务人违约的性质及影响（参见上文第2.3节）。

**中文:** 常见问题解答 问题3-1：在应用IFRS 9的减值要求时，主体是否必须纳入报告日与财务报表批准报出日之间发生的事项和预测？2015年4月，IFRS金融工具减值过渡资源小组（ITG）6讨论了在报告日应用IFRS 9减值要求时，是否以及如何纳入报告日与财务报表批准报出日之间发生的事项和预测。ITG指出，如果在报告日与财务报表签署日之间获得新信息，主体需要根据具体事实和情况运用判断，以确定该信息属于根据IAS 10的调整事项还是非调整事项。这一问题在第47章第5.9.4节中进一步讨论。 3.4 报告期后发现的舞弊行为 发现舞弊行为的时间点将影响会计处理考虑。当舞弊行为在报告期结束后、但在财务报表批准报出前被发现时，该舞弊行为的发现通常属于调整事项，即提供证据表明报告日已存在条件的事项（见上文第2.1.2节）。[IAS 10.9] 然而，舞弊行为的发现也可能属于非调整事项。例如，对于投资而言，舞弊行为的发现可能影响资产在发现后的公允价值，但不影响其在报告日的公允价值。当舞弊行为在财务报表批准报出后被发现时，这可能表明存在前期差错。在舞弊情况下应用IAS 8对"前期差错"的定义（见第3章第4.6节）需要运用判断。

**中文:** 必须评估相关事实和情况，以确定舞弊行为的发现是由于先前未能使用或误用可靠信息，还是由于在财务报表批准报出时已可获得、且合理预期应在财务报表编制过程中获取并考虑的新信息所致。[IAS 8.5, IAS 8(2027).5]。 3.5 对不确定税务处理估计的变更 IFRIC 23 阐述了如何在所得税会计中反映不确定性。该解释要求，若判断或估计所依据的事实和情况发生变化，或因新信息影响该判断或估计，主体应重新评估与不确定税务处理相关的任何判断或估计。若事实和情况的变化或新信息发生在报告期后，该解释要求主体应用 IAS 10 判断该变化是否构成调整事项或非调整事项，具体如上文第 2.1.2 节和第 2.1.3 节所述。**[IFRIC 23.13, IFRIC 23.14, IFRIC 23(2027).14]**。 参见第 47 章第 1.4 节“报告期后事项”。可能导致 IFRIC 23 所要求重新评估判断或估计的事实和情况变化或新信息的示例包括但不限于以下情形： (a) 税务当局的检查或行动。

**中文:** 例如： (i) 税务机关对主体采用的税务处理或类似税务处理表示同意或异议 (ii) 税务机关已对另一主体采用的类似税务处理表示同意或异议的信息 (iii) 为结算类似税务处理而收到或支付的金额信息 (b) 税务机关所制定规则的变更 (c) 税务机关审查或重新审查某项税务处理的权利到期 [IFRIC 23.A2]

**中文:** 报告期后税务机关所制定规则的变更构成非调整事项（见上文第2.1.3节）[IAS 10.22(h)]。主体必须运用《国际会计准则第10号》以确定报告期后发生的任何其他变更是调整事项还是非调整事项。IFRIC 23的要求将在第29章第9节进一步讨论。
