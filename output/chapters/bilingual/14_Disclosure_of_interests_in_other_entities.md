Frequently asked questions Appendix C - Defined terms Disclosure of interests in other entities Overview This Chapter discusses the requirements of IFRS 12 Disclosure of Interests in Other Entities. IFRS 12 is a disclosure standard that must be applied by entities that have an interest in subsidiaries, joint arrangements, associates, and unconsolidated structured entities. IFRS 12 does not provide any recognition or measurement guidance. The standard was issued as the disclosure requirements of certain standards overlapped in many areas and it was thought inappropriate that the disclosure requirements for interests in unconsolidated structured entities would be located in a consolidation standard. Therefore, a combined disclosure standard for interests in other entities would make it easier to understand and apply the disclosure requirements for subsidiaries, joint ventures, associates and unconsolidated structured entities. [IFRS 12.BC7]. In order to determine the appropriate recognition and measurement of subsidiaries, joint arrangements and associates, entities should refer to IFRS 10 Consolidated Financial Statements (see Chapter 7 and # Chapter 8), IFRS 11 Joint Arrangements (see Chapter 13), IAS 27 Separate Financial Statements (see # Chapter 9) and IAS 28 Investments in Associates and Joint Ventures (see Chapter 12) respectively. What you need to know • IFRS 12 requires an entity to disclose information that enables users of its financial statements to evaluate the nature of, and risks associated with, its interests in other entities and the effects of those interests on its financial position, financial performance and cash flows. • An interest in another entity refers to contractual and non-contractual involvement that exposes the entity to variable returns. • IFRS 12 contains disclosure requirements for interests in subsidiaries, joint arrangements and associates, as well as disclosures of interests in unconsolidated structured entities. Disclosure of interests in other entities Introduction IFRS 12 is a disclosure standard, issued in May 2011. It includes all of the disclosure requirements related to interests in subsidiaries, joint arrangements, associates and consolidated and unconsolidated structured entities. Differences between IFRS 12 and US GAAP disclosure requirements are not addressed in this chapter. The recognition and measurement of subsidiaries, joint arrangements and associates are dealt with in IFRS 10, IFRS 11, IAS 27 and IAS 28 (see Chapter 7, Chapter 8, Chapter 9, Chapter 12 and # Chapter 13 respectively). Disclosure of interests in other entities Objective and scope of IFRS 12 2.1 Objective The stated objective and how to meet the objective of IFRS 12 are as follows: [IFRS 12.1, IFRS 12.2]. Extract from IFRS 12 The objective of this IFRS is to require an entity to disclose information that enables users of its financial statements to evaluate: (a) the nature of, and risks associated with, its interests in other entities; and (b) the effects of those interests on its financial position, financial performance and cash flows. To meet the objective in paragraph 1, an entity shall disclose: (a) the significant judgements and assumptions it has made in determining: (i) the nature of its interest in another entity or arrangement; (ii) the type of joint arrangement in which it has an interest (paragraphs 7-9); (iii) that it meets the definition of an investment entity if applicable (paragraph 9A); and (b) information about its interests in: (i) subsidiaries (paragraphs 10-19); (ii) joint arrangements and associates (paragraphs 20-23); and (iii) structured entities that are not controlled by the entity (unconsolidated structured entities) (paragraphs 24-31). [IFRS 12.3]. Consideration of the purpose and design of the other entity may help the reporting entity when assessing whether it has an interest in that entity and, therefore, whether it is required to provide the disclosures in IFRS 12. That assessment must include consideration of the risks that the other entity was designed to create and the risks that the other entity was designed to pass on to the reporting entity and other parties. [IFRS 12.B7]. IFRS 10 defines ‘variability of returns’. As discussed in more detail in Chapter 7 at section 5, IFRS 10 explains that variable returns are returns that are not fixed and have the potential to vary as a result of the performance of an investee. Variable returns can be only positive, only negative or both positive and negative. An investor assesses whether returns from an investee are variable and how variable these returns are, on the basis of the substance of the arrangement and regardless of the legal form of the returns. For example, an investor can hold a bond with fixed interest payments. The fixed interest payments are variable returns for the purpose of IFRS 10 because they are subject to default risk and they expose the investor to the credit risk of the issuer of the bond. The amount of variability (i.e., how variable those returns are) depends on the credit risk of the bond. Similarly, fixed performance fees for managing an investee’s assets are variable returns because they expose the investor to the performance risk of the investee. The amount of variability depends on the investee’s ability to generate sufficient income to pay the fee. [IFRS 10.B56]. How we see it The definition of an ‘interest’ in IFRS 12 is much wider than only equity instruments in an entity. As IFRS 12 requires disclosures of interests that a reporting entity holds in other entities, preparers will need to ensure that their reporting systems and processes are sufficient to identify those ‘interests’. IFRS 12 clarifies that a reporting entity is typically exposed to variability of returns from the performance of another entity by holding instruments (such as equity or debt instruments issued by the other entity) or having another involvement that absorbs variability. [IFRS 12.B8]. This is illustrated in Illustration 2-1 below. Illustration 2-1: Variability of returns arising from issue of credit default swap (1) A reporting entity issues a credit default swap to a structured entity. The credit default swap protects the structured entity from the default of interest and principal payments on its loan portfolio. The reporting entity has an involvement in the structured entity that exposes it to variability of returns from the performance of the structured entity because the credit default swap absorbs variability of returns of the structured entity. [IFRS 12.B8]. Disclosure of interests in other entities Some instruments are designed to transfer risk from the reporting entity to another entity. Such instruments create variability of returns for the other entity but do not typically expose the reporting entity to variability of returns from the performance of the other entity. [IFRS 12.B9]. This is illustrated in Illustration 2-2 below. Illustration 2-2: Variability of returns arising from issue of credit default swap (2) A reporting entity (Entity Z) enters into a credit default swap with a structured entity. The credit default swap gives the structured entity exposure to Entity Z’s credit risk. The purpose of the arrangement is to give the investors in the structured entity exposure to Entity Z’s credit risk (Entity Z is unrelated to any other party involved in the arrangement). Entity Z does not have any involvement with the structured entity that exposes it to variable returns from the structured entity because the credit default swap transfers variability to the structured entity rather than absorbing variability of returns of the structured entity. Purchased call options and written put options (in each case unless the exercise price is at fair value) would also be interests in other entities, because these instruments typically absorb variability created by assets held in the entity. In contrast, some derivative instruments, such as interest rate swaps, can both create and absorb variability and judgement will need to be exercised in determining whether these derivatives are interests in other entities. How we see it We believe that plain vanilla swaps and other derivatives that both create and absorb variability, based on market rates or indices and which rank senior to the issued notes, do not absorb the risks the entity was designed to pass on, and are not an exposure to variable returns. They are therefore unlikely to be interests in other entities that would require disclosure under IFRS 12. See Chapter 7 at section 5.3.1. An entity does not necessarily have an interest in another entity because of a typical customer-supplier relationship. However, as explained above, IFRS 10 states that fixed performance fees for managing an investee’s assets create variable returns for the investor. The fixed performance fees are ‘variable’ because they expose the investor to the performance risk of the investee. [IFRS 10.B56]. Therefore, investment management fees and other fees based on assets under management are treated as variable interests. 2.2.1.B Structured entities Whether an entity is a structured entity or not is important because additional disclosures are required by IFRS 12 for interests in structured entities. These disclosures are discussed in section 4.4 and section 6 below. The IASB’s rationale for including specific disclosures of investments in structured entities is that users requested such disclosures because they believed involvement with these entities posed more risk than involvement with traditional operating entities. The increased risk exposure arises because, for example, the structured entity may have been created to pass risks and returns arising from specified assets to investors, or may have insufficient equity to fund losses on its assets, if they arise. [IFRS 12.BC84]. As defined in Appendix A of IFRS 12 (see Appendix C of this chapter), a structured entity is an entity that has been designed so that voting or similar rights are not the dominant factor in deciding who controls the entity, such as when any voting rights relate to administrative tasks only and the relevant activities are directed by means of contractual arrangements. IFRS 12 states that a structured entity often has some or all of the following features or attributes: (a) Restricted activities (b) A narrow and well-defined objective, such as: (i) To effect a tax-efficient lease (ii) To carry out research and development activities (iii) To provide a source of capital or funding to an entity Or (iv) To provide investment opportunities for investors by passing on risks and rewards associated with the assets of the structured entity to investors (c) Insufficient equity to permit the structured entity to finance its activities without subordinated financial support And (d) Financing in the form of multiple contractually linked instruments to investors that create concentrations of credit or other risks (tranches) [IFRS 12.B22] Disclosure of interests in other entities Appendix B of IFRS 12 provides the following examples of structured entities: [IFRS 12.B23] Extract from IFRS 12 B23 Examples of entities that are regarded as structured entities include, but are not limited to: (a) securitisation vehicles. (b) asset-backed financings. (c) some investment funds. The Basis for Conclusions explains that the type of entity the Board envisages being characterised as a structured entity is an entity created to accomplish a narrow and well-defined objective, listing as examples entities established to effect a lease, entities established for research and development activities or entities established for the securitisation of financial assets. [IFRS 12.BC82]. The IFRS 12 definition of a structured entity implies that any entity which is not controlled by voting or similar rights is a structured entity. Conversely, any entity controlled by voting or similar rights cannot be a structured entity. It is not clear what the IASB means by ‘similar’ (to voting) rights in the definition of a structured entity. No illustrative examples are provided. This will therefore require the exercise of judgement by reporting entities; consequently, there may be diversity in practice about what constitutes a ‘similar’ right and therefore, whether an entity is a structured entity. One example of ‘similar’ rights is an investment fund for which investors can vote to remove the manager of the fund without cause as long as a certain proportion of investors demand such a vote. The assessment of whether this right (to remove the fund manager) could be considered substantive, and therefore, whether the investment fund is a structured entity, would depend on the number of investors who would need to collaborate in order to force the vote, as well as other facts and circumstances. See Chapter 7 at section 4.1 and section 4.2 for additional guidance on identifying the significant activities of an entity, the rights to make decisions about those activities, and evaluating whether those rights are substantive. How we see it IFRS 12 does not state whether the ‘features or attributes’ of structured entities discussed above are determinative as to whether an entity is a structured entity or whether the features or attributes should always be subordinate to the definition (i.e., if the entity was controlled by voting or similar rights then the features or attributes would be irrelevant). Our view is that the features and attributes are subordinated to the definition. However, the features or attributes are often present in a structured entity. The IASB considered, but rejected, defining a structured entity in a way similar to a variable interest entity (VIE) in US GAAP. That approach, in the IASB’s opinion, would have introduced complicated guidance solely for disclosure purposes that was not previously in IFRS accounting standards. [IFRS 12.BC83]. US GAAP defines a VIE, in essence, as an entity whose activities are not directed through voting or similar rights, whose total equity is not sufficient to permit the entity to finance its activities without additional subordinated financial support, or whose equity is structured with non-substantive voting rights. The IASB had two reasons for not making the definition of a structured entity dependent on total equity at risk (as in US GAAP). First, including insufficient equity at risk in the definition of a structured entity would require extensive application guidance to help determine the sufficiency of the equity, to which the IASB was opposed. Second, the IASB feared that some traditional operating entities might be caught by this definition when this was not its intention. [IFRS 12.BC83, IFRS 12.BC84, IFRS 12.BC85]. IFRS 12 clarifies that an entity that is controlled by voting rights is not a structured entity simply because, for example, it receives funding from third parties following a restructuring. [IFRS 12.B24]. However, such funding is likely to give the investor a variable interest in the restructured entity that may still be a subsidiary as defined by IFRS 10. 2.2.1.C Interaction of IFRS 12 and IFRS 5 The requirements in IFRS 12, except as described in IFRS 12.B17, apply to an entity’s interests listed in IFRS 12.5 (see section 2.2 above) that are classified (or included in a disposal group that is classified) as held for sale or discontinued operations in accordance with IFRS 5 Non-current Assets Held for Sale and Discontinued Operations. The IASB decided not to provide such a definition of ‘loss’ but to leave it to the entity to identify what constitutes a loss in the particular context of that reporting entity. Therefore, the entity has to disclose how it has determined maximum loss exposure. The *Reference: IASB acknowledged that an entity might not always be able to calculate the maximum exposure to loss,* such as when a financial instrument exposes an entity to theoretically unlimited losses. The IASB decided that when this is the case an entity must disclose the reasons why it is not possible to calculate the maximum exposure to loss. [IFRS 12.BC98, IFRS 12.BC99]. How we see it In our view ‘maximum exposure to loss’ refers to the maximum loss that an entity could be required to record in its statement of comprehensive income as a result of its involvement with a structured entity. Further, this maximum possible loss must be disclosed regardless of the probability of such losses actually being incurred. IFRS 12 is silent on whether the maximum exposure is gross or net of collateral or hedging instruments held that would mitigate any loss. Consistent with the equivalent disclosures required by IFRS 7.36, we believe that the maximum exposure to loss should be disclosed gross of any collateral or hedging instruments and that separate disclosure should be made in respect of instruments held that would mitigate the loss on a net basis. The IASB also decided to require an entity to disclose a comparison of the carrying amounts of the assets and liabilities in its statement of financial position and its maximum exposure to loss. This is because the information will provide users with a better understanding of the differences between the maximum loss exposure and the expectation of whether it is likely that an entity will bear all or only some of the losses. The IASB reasoned that this information would help an entity explain why the maximum exposure to loss is unrepresentative of its actual exposure if that is the case. ## 在其他主体中权益的披露 概述 本章讨论IFRS 12《在其他主体中权益的披露》的要求。

**中文:** IFRS 12是一项披露准则，适用于持有子公司、合营安排、联营企业及未纳入合并范围的结构化主体权益的主体。该准则不提供任何确认或计量指引。由于某些准则的披露要求在多个领域存在重叠，且将未纳入合并范围的结构化主体权益的披露要求置于合并准则中并不恰当，因此该准则得以发布。为此，针对其他主体权益制定一项综合披露准则，将有助于更清晰地理解并应用对子公司、合营企业、联营企业及未纳入合并范围的结构化主体的披露要求。[IFRS 12.BC7]。为确定对子公司、合营安排及联营企业的适当确认与计量，主体应分别参照IFRS 10《合并财务报表》（见第7章及第8章）、IFRS 11《合营安排》（见第13章）、IAS 27《单独财务报表》（见第9章）及IAS 28《对联营企业和合营企业的投资》（见第12章）。

**中文:** 须知要点 • IFRS 12要求主体披露信息，以使财务报表使用者能够评估其在其他主体权益的性质及相关风险，以及这些权益对其财务状况、财务业绩和现金流量的影响。 • 其他主体权益指使主体面临可变回报风险的合同性及非合同性参与。 • IFRS 12包含对子公司、合营安排及联营企业权益的披露要求，以及对未纳入合并范围的结构化主体权益的披露。

## 其他主体权益的披露 引言 IFRS 12是一项披露准则，于2011年5月发布。

**中文:** 它包括所有与子公司、合营安排、联营企业以及合并和未合并结构化实体权益相关的披露要求。本章不涉及国际财务报告准则第12号与美国公认会计原则披露要求之间的差异。子公司、合营安排和联营企业的确认与计量由《国际财务报告准则第10号》、《国际财务报告准则第11号》、《国际会计准则第27号》和《国际会计准则第28号》规范（分别参见第7章、第8章、第9章、第12章及第13章）。 **在其他实体中权益的披露** **国际财务报告准则第12号的目标与范围** **2.1 目标** 《国际财务报告准则第12号》明确的目标及实现该目标的方式如下：[IFRS 12.1, IFRS 12.2]。 **摘自《国际财务报告准则第12号》** 本准则的目标是要求主体披露信息，以使财务报表使用者能够评估： (a) 其在其他实体中权益的性质及相关风险； (b) 这些权益对其财务状况、财务业绩和现金流量的影响。 为实现第1段所述目标，主体应披露： (a) 在确定以下事项时所作出的重大判断和假设： (i) 其在其他实体或安排中权益的性质； (ii) 其拥有权益的合营安排类型（第7-9段）； (iii) 其是否符合投资性主体的定义（如适用）（第9A段）； (b) 关于其在以下实体中权益的信息： (i) 子公司（第10-19段）； (ii) 合营安排和联营企业（第20-23段）； (iii) 不受主体控制的结构化实体（未合并结构化实体）（第24-31段）。 若本准则要求的披露，连同其他国际财务报告会计准则要求的披露，未能实现《国际财务报告准则第12号》的目标，主体必须披露为实现该目标所必需的任何额外信息。[IFRS 12.3]。

**中文:** 该要求重申了《国际会计准则第1号——财务报表列报》中已明确的原则，即当有助于理解财务报表时，应披露额外信息。[IAS 1.17(c)、IAS 1.31、IAS 1.112(c)]。1 我们的理解是，《国际财务报告准则第12号》未提供任何示例以支持其披露要求。此外，该准则中多项术语未予定义。因此，主体在解释相关要求时需运用判断。2.1.1 中期财务报告 《国际会计准则第34号——中期财务报告》（见第37章）允许主体在中期报告中列报整套财务报表，或按实务惯例列报简明财务报表。[IAS 34.4]。在中期财务报表中，主体必须披露中期内对理解自上一年度报告期以来财务状况和业绩变化具有重要影响的事项和交易信息（见第37章第4.1节）。[IAS 34.15]。此外，中期财务报表附注或中期财务报告其他部分需披露若干特定信息（见第37章第4.2节）。[IAS 34.16A]。例如，根据《国际财务报告准则第10号》定义成为或不再作为投资性主体的主体，须披露《国际财务报告准则第12号》第9B段要求的信息（见下文第4.6.2节）。[IAS 34.16A(k)]。同时，还需披露中期内主体构成变动、取得或丧失子公司及长期投资控制权的影响相关信息。[IAS 34.16A(i)]。《国际会计准则第34号》要求的披露适用于中期列报的完整财务报表和简明财务报表。

**中文:** 该评估必须包括对其他实体被设计所产生风险，以及该实体被设计向报告主体及其他方转移风险的考量。[IFRS 12.B7]。IFRS 10定义了"回报可变性"。如第7章第5节详述，IFRS 10解释称，可变回报是指非固定且可能因被投资方业绩而变动的回报。可变回报可能仅为正值、仅为负值，或同时包含正负值。投资者应根据安排的实质（无论回报的法律形式如何）评估被投资方回报是否可变及其变动程度。例如，投资者持有固定利息支付的债券。就IFRS 10而言，该固定利息支付属于可变回报，因其存在违约风险，使投资者面临债券发行方的信用风险。变动程度（即回报的可变幅度）取决于债券的信用风险。类似地，管理被投资方资产的固定业绩报酬也属于可变回报，因其使投资者面临被投资方的业绩风险。变动程度取决于被投资方产生足够收入以支付报酬的能力。[IFRS 10.B56]。我们的观点 IFRS 12中"权益"的定义远宽泛于主体中的权益工具。由于IFRS 12要求披露报告主体在其他实体中持有的权益，编制者需确保其报告系统及流程足以识别这些"权益"。IFRS 12明确，报告主体通常通过持有工具（如其他主体发行的权益或债务工具）或通过其他吸收可变性的参与方式，面临因其他实体业绩产生的回报可变性。

**中文:** IFRS IC指出，报告主体在编制对自身重要的合营企业或联营企业的汇总财务信息时，若该合营企业或联营企业拥有子公司，应基于其合并财务报表进行列报；若其无子公司，则应基于其自身财务报表（其中包含采用权益法核算的合营企业或联营企业）进行列报。IFRS IC认为，这些观点与IFRS 12.B14(a)一致，该条款要求合营企业或联营企业财务报表中的金额必须进行调整，以反映报告主体采用权益法所作的调整，例如收购时的公允价值调整以及会计政策差异调整。因此，IFRS IC决定无需制定解释或修订准则，并决定不将该问题纳入其议程。11 准则未明确汇总财务信息与主体在合营企业或联营企业投资账面金额的调节表中应包含哪些组成部分。根据IFRS IC的澄清，合营企业或联营企业IFRS财务报表中的金额必须按照IFRS 12.B14(a)的要求进行调整，以反映公允价值及会计政策调整。因此，在成为报告主体合营企业或联营企业时，其账面中存在的任何原有商誉必须作为公允价值调整从IFRS 12.B14(a)的金额中剔除。我们的观点 关于报告主体归属于该合营企业或联营企业且已计入投资账面金额的商誉所产生的影响，尚不明确。

**中文:** 此类商誉可作为《国际财务报告准则第12号》B14(a)段规定的公允价值调整纳入汇总财务信息，或作为B14(b)段规定的调节项目纳入。因此，需审慎说明汇总国际财务报告准则财务信息如何与报告主体在合营企业或联营企业中的权益账面金额进行调节。若满足以下条件，主体可基于合营企业或联营企业的财务报表列报所要求的汇总财务信息：

**中文:** 国际会计准则理事会认为有必要披露主体的最大损失敞口，因其担心若仅要求披露预期损失信息，主体可能常会识别出其在未纳入合并范围的结构化主体中的权益具有正预期回报值，从而不会披露任何损失敞口。[《国际财务报告准则第12号》结论基础第97段]。《国际财务报告准则第12号》未定义最大损失敞口。国际会计准则理事会决定不提供此类“损失”定义，而是由主体根据该报告主体的具体背景自行确定何为损失。因此，主体须披露其如何确定最大损失敞口。 *参考：国际会计准则理事会承认，主体可能并非总能计算最大损失敞口，* 例如当金融工具使主体面临理论上无上限的损失时。
